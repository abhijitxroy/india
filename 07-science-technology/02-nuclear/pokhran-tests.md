# Pokhran Tests

## Overview

The Pokhran Tests were a series of nuclear weapon tests conducted by India at the Pokhran Test Range in Rajasthan.

These tests marked important milestones in India's nuclear programme and demonstrated the country's nuclear capabilities.

The tests were conducted in two phases:

- Pokhran-I (1974)
- Pokhran-II (1998)

## Pokhran Test Range

The tests were conducted at:

- Pokhran, Rajasthan

The location was selected because of its remote desert environment and strategic suitability.

## Pokhran-I (1974)

### Date

- 18 May 1974

### Code Name

- Smiling Buddha

### Prime Minister

- Indira Gandhi

### Significance

Pokhran-I was India's first nuclear test.

The government described it as a Peaceful Nuclear Explosion (PNE).

### Impact

- Demonstrated India's nuclear capability
- Increased global attention on India's nuclear programme
- Led to restrictions on nuclear technology transfers

## Pokhran-II (1998)

### Dates

- 11 May 1998
- 13 May 1998

### Operation Name

- Operation Shakti

### Prime Minister

- Atal Bihari Vajpayee

### Tests Conducted

India conducted multiple underground nuclear tests during this phase.

The tests included:

- Fission devices
- Thermonuclear device
- Sub-kiloton devices

### Objectives

- Demonstrate nuclear weapon capability
- Strengthen national security
- Establish credible nuclear deterrence

## Key Scientists

## International Reactions

The tests attracted significant international attention.

Major consequences included:

- Economic sanctions by some countries
- Diplomatic discussions on nuclear policy
- Increased focus on non-proliferation issues

## Impact on India's Nuclear Policy

The Pokhran-II tests led to the formal evolution of India's nuclear doctrine.

Key principles included:

- Credible Minimum Deterrence
- No First Use (NFU)
- Civilian political control over nuclear weapons

## Nuclear Doctrine

India's nuclear doctrine emphasizes:

- Responsible nuclear stewardship
- Deterrence rather than aggression
- Strategic stability

## Strategic Importance

The Pokhran tests:

- Strengthened India's strategic position
- Enhanced national security capabilities
- Established India as a nuclear weapons state in practice
- Influenced defence and foreign policy planning

## Related Institutions

Important institutions associated with India's nuclear programme include:

- Department of Atomic Energy (DAE)
- Bhabha Atomic Research Centre (BARC)
- Defence Research and Development Organisation (DRDO)
- Atomic Energy Commission (AEC)

## Challenges and Debates

Important issues discussed after the tests included:

- Nuclear arms control
- Regional security concerns
- Strategic deterrence
- International non-proliferation frameworks
