# 05 — Mutually Assured Destruction (MAD) Explained

**Timestamp:** 21:51

## 1. What MAD means

**Mutually Assured Destruction (MAD)** is the idea that a nuclear attack by one major nuclear power would result in the destruction of both sides.

The basic logic is:

1. Country A launches nuclear missiles.
2. Country B detects the incoming attack before its own missiles are destroyed.
3. Country B launches a retaliatory strike.
4. Country A is destroyed by the retaliation.
5. Therefore, launching the first strike does not produce victory.

The word “mutually” is essential: both sides possess enough capability to destroy the other.

## 2. Example used in the discussion

The speaker uses the United States and Russia as the example.

If the United States launched missiles against Russia:

- Russia would detect the incoming missiles.
- Russia would have time to launch its own missiles.
- Even if the original US attack destroyed Russian cities or launch facilities, Russian missiles would already be on their way.
- Those Russian missiles would then destroy the United States.

The result is mutual destruction.

## 3. Why this creates deterrence

The purpose of nuclear deterrence is therefore not necessarily to use the weapon.

The weapon's strategic value comes from the certainty that an attacker will face devastating retaliation.

The logic becomes:

> If you attack me, you cannot survive the consequences.

This creates a strategic incentive not to launch the first attack.

## 4. Why second-strike capability matters

MAD depends heavily on the ability to retaliate after absorbing an initial attack.

If an opponent could destroy every nuclear weapon before they could be launched, deterrence would become weaker.

Therefore, countries seek survivable nuclear forces.

This leads directly into India's nuclear triad and submarine-based deterrence discussed later.
