# Chapter 09 --- India vs China: Nuclear Power & Thorium Race

**Timestamp:** 42:50--45:24

## Complete Notes

-   China is described as far ahead of India in nuclear power in terms
    of:
    -   reactor numbers;
    -   total capacity;
    -   electricity output;
    -   construction scale.
-   India is described as having roughly 20--22 nuclear reactors in the
    context of the discussion.
-   China is described as having plans for more than 100 additional
    reactors.
-   China is characterized as highly ambitious and capable of operating
    at very large scale.
-   India's programme is described as progressing more gradually.
-   The major Indian strategic advantage identified is thorium.
-   The podcast states that India has approximately 1.07 million tonnes
    of thorium.
-   China is described as lacking a comparable thorium resource.
-   The host asks whether this could allow India to change the balance
    over 20--30 years despite currently being behind.
-   The speaker says there is a realistic possibility if India is
    sufficiently ambitious and executes properly.
-   A target of approximately 20--25 Stage 2 reactors by 2050 is
    suggested.
-   Stage 1 reactors should continue operating and could also be
    expanded.
-   Stage 1 expansion is constrained by India's limited uranium.
-   Stage 2 is different because breeder reactors can multiply the
    available fissile-fuel inventory.
-   Therefore, the number of Stage 2 reactors built by 2050 is presented
    as a critical factor.
-   If India succeeds in scaling Stage 2, the speaker believes India has
    the potential to catch China and eventually move ahead in the Stage
    3 thorium domain.
-   The speaker emphasizes that potential alone is not sufficient.
    Success depends on ambition, execution and sustained construction.

## Key Takeaway

> India is currently behind China in nuclear scale, but the podcast
> argues that India's thorium resource and successful execution of Stage
> 2 could create a different long-term competitive advantage.
