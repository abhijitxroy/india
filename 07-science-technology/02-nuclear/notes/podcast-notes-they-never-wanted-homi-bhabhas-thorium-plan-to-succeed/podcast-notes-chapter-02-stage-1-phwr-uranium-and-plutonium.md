# Chapter 02 --- Stage 1: PHWR, Uranium & Plutonium

**Timestamp:** 04:58--10:07

## Complete Notes

-   Stage 1 begins with the problem that India does not have abundant
    enriched uranium.
-   Naturally occurring uranium contains several isotopes.
-   The discussion emphasizes U-235 as fissile and U-238 as fertile.
-   The transcript states that natural uranium contains approximately
    0.7% U-235 and 99.3% U-238.
-   Increasing the proportion of U-235 is called uranium enrichment.
-   The discussion says weapons-grade uranium requires roughly 90% or
    more U-235.
-   India therefore plans to use naturally occurring uranium rather than
    depending on large quantities of enriched uranium.
-   Stage 1 uses **Pressurized Heavy Water Reactors (PHWRs)**.
-   PHWRs use natural uranium as fuel.
-   The major strategic output of Stage 1 is plutonium, particularly
    plutonium-239.
-   Plutonium-239 is described as an important fissile material.
-   It can be used as nuclear fuel for electricity generation and can
    also be used in nuclear weapons.
-   The purpose of Stage 1 is therefore twofold:
    -   generate electricity;
    -   build a plutonium inventory for Stage 2.
-   The host summarizes the fuel-cycle idea by asking whether U-238 can
    eventually be converted into Pu-239 in a fast breeder reactor.
-   The answer leads directly into Stage 2.
-   The programme therefore does not treat each stage as an isolated
    reactor technology. Each stage produces important material needed by
    the next stage.

## Fuel-Cycle Summary

``` text
Natural uranium
      ↓
PHWR
      ↓
Electricity + plutonium
      ↓
Plutonium inventory
      ↓
Stage 2 fuel
```

## Key Takeaway

> Stage 1 is the foundation: India uses natural uranium in PHWRs while
> building the plutonium inventory required for the breeder-reactor
> stage.
