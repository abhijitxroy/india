

# Municipality

## Overview

A Municipality is a form of urban local self-government established for medium-sized towns and urban areas.

Municipalities are responsible for providing civic services, urban infrastructure, local development, and public welfare.

They function between Nagar Panchayats and Municipal Corporations in the urban governance framework.

## Importance

Municipalities are important because they:

- Deliver essential civic services
- Support planned urban growth
- Improve local infrastructure
- Promote local democracy
- Address community needs
- Implement development programmes

## Constitutional Basis

The 74th Constitutional Amendment Act, 1992 provided constitutional status to urban local bodies.

Municipalities form one of the key institutions of urban local governance.

## Position in Urban Governance

```text
Municipal Corporation
      ↓
Municipality
      ↓
Nagar Panchayat
```

## Composition

A Municipality generally consists of:

- Chairperson or Municipal Chairperson
- Elected councillors
- Municipal committees
- Administrative officers

Councillors represent different wards within the town.

## Major Functions

### Civic Services

- Water supply
- Sanitation
- Waste management
- Street lighting
- Public health services

### Infrastructure Development

- Local roads
- Drainage systems
- Public buildings
- Markets and community facilities

### Urban Planning

- Building approvals
- Land-use regulation
- Local development planning

### Public Welfare

- Health initiatives
- Educational support programmes
- Community welfare projects

## Sources of Revenue

Municipalities generate revenue through:

- Property tax
- Fees and user charges
- State government grants
- Central government grants
- Licences and permits

## Challenges

Common challenges include:

- Rapid urban expansion
- Limited financial resources
- Infrastructure maintenance
- Waste management
- Environmental concerns
