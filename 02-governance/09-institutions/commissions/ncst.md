# National Commission for Scheduled Tribes (NCST)

## Overview

The National Commission for Scheduled Tribes (NCST) is a constitutional body established to protect the rights, welfare, and development of Scheduled Tribes (STs) in India.

The commission monitors constitutional safeguards, investigates complaints, and advises governments on matters affecting tribal communities.

## Constitutional Status

The NCST is a constitutional body established under:

- Article 338A of the Constitution of India

## Establishment

### Background

Originally, Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes were covered under a combined commission.

### Separate Commission

The Constitution (89th Amendment) Act, 2003 created a separate commission for Scheduled Tribes.

The NCST came into existence in 2004.

## Composition

The commission consists of:

- Chairperson
- Vice-Chairperson
- Three Other Members

All members are appointed by the President of India.

## Functions

### Monitor Safeguards

The commission monitors safeguards provided to Scheduled Tribes under:

- Constitution
- Laws
- Government programmes
- Welfare schemes

### Investigate Complaints

The commission investigates complaints related to:

- Violation of tribal rights
- Land alienation
- Displacement
- Exploitation
- Non-implementation of safeguards

### Advise Governments

The commission advises the Union and State Governments on tribal welfare and development policies.

### Reporting

The commission submits reports to the President regarding implementation of safeguards for Scheduled Tribes.

## Powers

The NCST has powers similar to a civil court during investigations.

It can:

- Summon individuals
- Examine witnesses
- Call for records
- Receive evidence
- Conduct inquiries

## Areas of Focus

Important focus areas include:

- Tribal rights
- Forest rights
- Education
- Healthcare
- Livelihoods
- Land rights
- Rehabilitation and resettlement

## Forest Rights

The commission plays an important role in monitoring implementation of:

- Scheduled Tribes and Other Traditional Forest Dwellers (Recognition of Forest Rights) Act, 2006

This law is commonly known as the Forest Rights Act (FRA).

## Significance

The NCST helps:

- Protect tribal communities
- Preserve tribal interests
- Promote inclusive development
- Monitor welfare programmes
- Strengthen constitutional safeguards

## Challenges

Major challenges include:

- Land displacement
- Development-induced migration
- Forest-related disputes
- Poverty
- Educational gaps
- Healthcare access issues

## Difference Between NCSC and NCST

| Feature | NCSC | NCST |
|----------|----------|----------|
| Constitutional Article | Article 338 | Article 338A |
| Focus Group | Scheduled Castes | Scheduled Tribes |
| Key Issues | Social Justice and Representation | Tribal Rights and Development |
