# Central Vigilance Commission (CVC)

## Overview

The Central Vigilance Commission (CVC) is the apex vigilance institution of India.

It was established to prevent corruption, promote integrity in public administration, and supervise vigilance activities in Central Government organizations.

The commission functions independently and plays a key role in India's anti-corruption framework.

## Legal Basis

The CVC is a statutory body established under:

- Central Vigilance Commission Act, 2003

## Historical Background

The CVC was originally established in 1964 on the recommendations of the Santhanam Committee on Prevention of Corruption.

It later received statutory status through the Central Vigilance Commission Act, 2003.

## Composition

The commission consists of:

- Central Vigilance Commissioner (Chairperson)
- Up to Two Vigilance Commissioners

Members are appointed by the President of India.

## Appointment Committee

Appointments are made on the recommendation of a committee consisting of:

- Prime Minister (Chairperson)
- Union Home Minister
- Leader of Opposition in Lok Sabha

## Functions

### Anti-Corruption Oversight

The commission supervises vigilance administration in Central Government organizations.

### Advisory Role

The CVC advises government departments on vigilance and disciplinary matters.

### Supervision of Investigations

The commission exercises superintendence over investigations related to corruption conducted by the CBI under the Prevention of Corruption framework.

### Monitoring Vigilance Activities

The commission reviews vigilance mechanisms across ministries, departments, public sector undertakings, and other organizations.

## Powers

The CVC can:

- Conduct inquiries
- Review corruption cases
- Recommend disciplinary action
- Monitor vigilance administration
- Seek reports from departments and agencies

However, its recommendations are generally advisory in nature.

## Vigilance Administration

Each ministry and public sector organization maintains vigilance units.

These units work with the CVC to:

- Detect corruption
- Prevent misconduct
- Promote ethical administration

## Importance

The CVC helps:

- Promote integrity in governance
- Reduce corruption risks
- Improve accountability
- Strengthen public trust in institutions
- Enhance administrative transparency

## Related Institutions

Important institutions associated with anti-corruption efforts include:

- Central Bureau of Investigation (CBI)
- Lokpal
- Central Information Commission (CIC)
- Enforcement Directorate (ED)

## Challenges

Major challenges include:

- Complex corruption cases
- Delays in investigations
- Coordination among agencies
- Growing financial crimes
- Implementation of recommendations

## Difference Between CIC and CVC

| Feature | CIC | CVC |
|----------|----------|----------|
| Focus | Transparency and Information Access | Vigilance and Anti-Corruption |
| Legal Basis | RTI Act, 2005 | CVC Act, 2003 |
| Main Function | RTI Appeals and Complaints | Vigilance Oversight |
| Objective | Transparency | Integrity in Administration |
