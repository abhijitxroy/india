# Union Public Service Commission (UPSC)

## Overview

The Union Public Service Commission (UPSC) is India's premier central recruiting agency responsible for conducting examinations and selecting candidates for various civil services and central government posts.

It plays a crucial role in ensuring a merit-based, impartial, and professional civil service system.

## Constitutional Status

The UPSC is a constitutional body established under:

- Articles 315 to 323 of the Constitution of India

## Establishment

The UPSC came into existence on 26 January 1950.

It succeeded the Federal Public Service Commission established during British rule.

## Composition

The commission consists of:

- Chairperson
- Other Members

Members are appointed by the President of India.

## Tenure

A member of the UPSC holds office for:

- Six years, or
- Until attaining the age of 65 years

Whichever is earlier.

## Functions

### Conduct Examinations

The UPSC conducts examinations for recruitment to:

- All India Services
- Central Civil Services
- Various Central Government posts

### Recruitment by Selection

The commission assists in direct recruitment through interviews and selection processes.

### Advisory Role

The UPSC advises the Government on:

- Recruitment methods
- Promotions
- Transfers
- Disciplinary matters
- Service rules

### Personnel Management

The commission provides recommendations regarding public service administration.

## Major Examinations Conducted by UPSC

Important examinations include:

- Civil Services Examination (CSE)
- Engineering Services Examination (ESE)
- Combined Defence Services Examination (CDS)
- National Defence Academy Examination (NDA)
- Indian Forest Service Examination (IFoS)
- Combined Medical Services Examination (CMS)

## Independence of UPSC

The Constitution provides safeguards to ensure the independence of the commission.

These include:

- Security of tenure
- Fixed service conditions
- Protection regarding removal from office

## Removal of Members

The President can remove a member under specific constitutional provisions.

In certain cases, consultation with the Supreme Court is required.

## Importance

The UPSC helps:

- Maintain merit-based recruitment
- Ensure administrative neutrality
- Strengthen governance
- Build a professional civil service
- Promote equal opportunity in public employment

## Challenges

Major challenges include:

- Large number of applicants
- Evolving governance requirements
- Balancing specialization and general administration
- Adapting recruitment to emerging sectors

## Difference Between UPSC and State Public Service Commissions

| Feature | UPSC | State PSC |
|----------|----------|----------|
| Jurisdiction | Union Government | State Government |
| Constitutional Basis | Articles 315–323 | Articles 315–323 |
| Recruitment | Central Services | State Services |
| Appointing Authority | President of India | Governor |
