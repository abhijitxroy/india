# Attorney General of India

## Overview

The Attorney General of India is the highest law officer of the Government of India.

The Attorney General advises the Union Government on legal matters and represents the Government of India in courts.

The office plays an important role in the administration of justice and constitutional governance.

## Constitutional Status

The Attorney General is established under:

- Article 76 of the Constitution of India

## Appointment

The Attorney General is appointed by the President of India.

The person appointed must be qualified to be appointed as a Judge of the Supreme Court.

## Qualifications

A person must:

- Be a citizen of India
- Have served as a High Court judge for at least five years, or
- Have been an advocate of a High Court for at least ten years, or
- Be considered a distinguished jurist by the President

## Term of Office

The Constitution does not specify a fixed tenure.

The Attorney General holds office during the pleasure of the President.

## Functions

### Legal Advisor to the Government

The Attorney General advises the Union Government on legal and constitutional matters.

### Representation in Courts

The Attorney General represents the Government of India before:

- Supreme Court
- High Courts
- Other judicial forums

### Constitutional Duties

The Attorney General performs legal duties assigned by the President.

### Advice on Legislation

The Attorney General may provide legal opinions on proposed laws and government actions.

## Rights and Privileges

The Attorney General has the right to:

- Participate in proceedings of Parliament
- Participate in Parliamentary Committee proceedings
- Speak in Parliament

However, the Attorney General does not have the right to vote.

## Position in Government

The Attorney General is not a member of the Union Cabinet.

The office is considered a constitutional position rather than a political office.

## Importance

The Attorney General helps:

- Protect constitutional governance
- Defend government actions in courts
- Provide expert legal advice
- Support the rule of law
- Ensure legal compliance in administration

## Limitations

The Attorney General is not a full-time government employee.

Certain restrictions apply regarding private legal practice to avoid conflicts of interest.

## Difference Between Attorney General and Solicitor General

| Feature | Attorney General | Solicitor General |
|----------|----------|----------|
| Status | Constitutional Office | Statutory/Executive Appointment |
| Constitutional Article | Article 76 | No Constitutional Status |
| Position | Highest Law Officer | Second Highest Law Officer |
| Appointed By | President of India | Government of India |
