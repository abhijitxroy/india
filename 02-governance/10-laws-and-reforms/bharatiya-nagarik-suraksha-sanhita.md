# Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita (BNSS)

## Overview

The Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita (BNSS), 2023 is India's new criminal procedure law. It replaced the Code of Criminal Procedure (CrPC), 1973 and came into force on 1 July 2024.

BNSS provides the procedural framework for investigation, arrest, bail, trial, evidence collection, and administration of criminal justice in India.

## Background

The Government of India introduced three major criminal law reforms:

- Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita (BNS)
- Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita (BNSS)
- Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam (BSA)

Together, these laws replaced the colonial-era criminal law framework.

## Purpose

The objectives of BNSS include:

- Modernization of criminal procedure
- Faster delivery of justice
- Greater use of technology
- Improved victim-centric processes
- Reduction of procedural delays

## Major Features

### Use of Technology

BNSS promotes:

- Electronic records
- Digital documentation
- Electronic communication of notices and summons
- Audio-video recording in investigations where applicable

### Timelines for Investigation and Trial

The law introduces timelines for various stages of criminal proceedings to improve efficiency.

### Victim-Centric Approach

Key provisions strengthen:

- Rights of victims
- Information sharing during investigations
- Participation in the justice process

### Forensic Investigation

BNSS increases the role of forensic science in criminal investigations, especially for serious offences.

## Arrest and Detention

BNSS contains provisions regarding:

- Arrest procedures
- Rights of arrested persons
- Police custody
- Judicial custody
- Bail procedures

The law aims to balance investigation requirements with protection of individual rights.

## Investigation Process

Important areas include:

- Registration of offences
- Collection of evidence
- Search and seizure procedures
- Statements and documentation
- Use of forensic evidence

## Trial Process

BNSS governs:

- Criminal courts
- Conduct of trials
- Summons and warrants
- Examination of witnesses
- Appeals and related procedures

## Significance

The law is significant because it:

- Replaces a colonial-era procedural framework
- Introduces technology-driven processes
- Encourages faster justice delivery
- Strengthens forensic investigation mechanisms
- Forms part of India's criminal justice reforms
