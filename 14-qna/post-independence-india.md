# India Post-Independence History — Q&A

## 1. What was the Emergency of 1975–77?

The **Emergency** was a period from **25 June 1975 to 21 March 1977** when Prime Minister **Indira Gandhi** invoked special constitutional emergency powers.

During this period:

* Opposition leaders were arrested.
* Press censorship was imposed.
* Civil liberties were restricted.
* Political activities and protests were heavily controlled.
* Elections were postponed.

The Emergency ended in March 1977. Elections were held, Congress lost, and the **Janata Party** came to power.

---

## 2. Who was Prime Minister during the Emergency?

**Indira Gandhi** was Prime Minister throughout the Emergency.

```text
  ↓

  ↓
1977
  ↓
Emergency ends
  ↓
General Election
  ↓
Congress loses
  ↓
Janata Party wins
```

## 3. What was the License Raj?

**License Raj** was a system of extensive government control over private businesses and industries.

Companies often needed government licences and approvals to:

* Start an industrial unit
* Expand production
* Increase capacity
* Import equipment
* Obtain foreign exchange
* Manufacture certain products

### Simple example

```text
Business wants to expand
        ↓
Government permission
        ↓
Industrial licence
        ↓
Production approval
        ↓
Import / foreign-exchange approval
        ↓
Expansion
```

The objective was to control scarce resources and guide India's industrial development.

Over time, License Raj became associated with:

* Bureaucracy
* Delays
* Red tape
* Corruption
* Low competition
* Inefficiency

It was substantially dismantled through the **1991 economic reforms**.

---

## 4. Why did India have the License Raj?

After independence, India was:

* Poor
* Short of capital
* Short of foreign exchange
* Dependent on imports
* Trying to build domestic industries

The government therefore adopted a **planned, state-led economic model**.

The basic idea was:

> The government should guide what industries were created, how much they produced, and where resources went.

## 5. What was the 1973 Oil Crisis?

The **1973 Oil Crisis** was a major global increase in oil prices following the Arab-Israeli conflict and an Arab oil embargo.

India depended heavily on imported oil, so the price increase created significant economic pressure.

```text
Oil prices ↑
    ↓
India's import bill ↑
    ↓
Foreign-exchange pressure ↑
    ↓
Inflation ↑
    ↓
Transport & production costs ↑
    ↓
Economic pressure
```

This contributed to the difficult economic environment of the 1970s.

---

## 6. What does "Post-1973 Oil Crisis" mean?

It refers to the period after the 1973 global oil shock when India had to deal with:

* Higher oil-import costs
* Inflation
* Foreign-exchange pressure
* Higher transportation costs
* Higher production costs
* Slower economic growth

It is an important part of understanding India's economic difficulties during the 1970s.

## 7. What was the Green Revolution?

The **Green Revolution** was the transformation of Indian agriculture through modern farming technology and methods.

It involved:

* High-Yielding Variety (HYV) seeds
* Irrigation
* Chemical fertilisers
* Pesticides
* Better farming techniques
* Mechanisation
* Government support policies

The main objective was:

> **Increase food production and achieve food security.**

```text
HYV seeds
+ Irrigation
+ Fertilisers
+ Technology
        ↓
Higher crop yields
        ↓
Higher food production
        ↓
Greater food security
```

It was particularly successful with **wheat and rice**.

---

## 8. Why was the Green Revolution important for India?

Before the Green Revolution, India frequently faced food shortages and depended heavily on food imports.

The Green Revolution helped India move toward:

> **Food self-sufficiency and greater food security.**

It also reduced India's vulnerability to external food supplies.

---

## 9. What was the White Revolution?

The **White Revolution** was the transformation of India's dairy sector.

Its objectives were to:

* Increase milk production
* Connect farmers with organised dairy markets
* Increase rural incomes
* Build dairy cooperatives
* Improve milk distribution

The White Revolution is strongly associated with **Operation Flood**.

---

## 10. What was Operation Flood?

**Operation Flood** was India's major dairy development programme launched in **1970**.

It was implemented by the **National Dairy Development Board (NDDB)**.

The programme connected millions of milk producers through cooperative organisations.

```text
Small dairy farmers
        ↓
Milk cooperatives
        ↓
Milk collection
        ↓
Processing
        ↓
Distribution
        ↓
Consumers
```

Operation Flood became the foundation of India's **White Revolution**.

---

## 11. What is the relationship between Operation Flood and the White Revolution?

They are closely connected:

```text
Operation Flood
       ↓
Dairy cooperatives + better milk distribution
       ↓
Higher milk production
       ↓
White Revolution
```

**Operation Flood = Programme**

**White Revolution = Transformation/result**

---

## 12. What is the difference between the Green Revolution and White Revolution?

| Revolution       | Sector      | Main focus | Main objective                             |
| ---------------- | ----------- | ---------- | ------------------------------------------ |
| Green Revolution | Agriculture | Crops      | Food security                              |
| White Revolution | Dairy       | Milk       | Increase milk production and farmer income |

# Political Timeline

## 13. Who was India's first Prime Minister?

**Jawaharlal Nehru** became India's first Prime Minister on **15 August 1947**.

He remained Prime Minister until **27 May 1964**.

His tenure was approximately **17 years**, not 7 years.

---

---

## 14. What is the correct PM timeline from 1947 to today?

India has had **15 Prime Ministers** so far. However, there have been **18 separate periods of Prime Ministerial office**, because Gulzarilal Nanda served twice as interim Prime Minister, while Indira Gandhi and Atal Bihari Vajpayee each had multiple separate terms.

### Complete Timeline

| Period | Prime Minister | Political Party | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **15 Aug 1947 – 27 May 1964** | **Jawaharlal Nehru** | Congress | India's first PM; ~17 years |
| **27 May 1964 – 9 Jun 1964** | **Gulzarilal Nanda** | Congress | Interim PM |
| **9 Jun 1964 – 11 Jan 1966** | **Lal Bahadur Shastri** | Congress | PM during the 1965 India-Pakistan War |
| **11 Jan 1966 – 24 Jan 1966** | **Gulzarilal Nanda** | Congress | Interim PM |
| **24 Jan 1966 – 24 Mar 1977** | **Indira Gandhi** | Congress | Emergency occurred during this tenure |
| **24 Mar 1977 – 28 Jul 1979** | **Morarji Desai** | Janata Party | First non-Congress PM |
| **28 Jul 1979 – 14 Jan 1980** | **Charan Singh** | Janata Party | Short-lived government |
| **14 Jan 1980 – 31 Oct 1984** | **Indira Gandhi** | Congress | Returned as PM |
| **31 Oct 1984 – 2 Dec 1989** | **Rajiv Gandhi** | Congress | Became PM after Indira Gandhi's assassination |
| **2 Dec 1989 – 10 Nov 1990** | **V. P. Singh** | Janata Dal | National Front government |
| **10 Nov 1990 – 21 Jun 1991** | **Chandra Shekhar** | Janata Dal (Socialist) | Government supported by Congress |
| **21 Jun 1991 – 16 May 1996** | **P. V. Narasimha Rao** | Congress | 1991 economic reforms |
| **16 May 1996 – 1 Jun 1996** | **Atal Bihari Vajpayee** | BJP | First Vajpayee government; 13 days |
| **1 Jun 1996 – 21 Apr 1997** | **H. D. Deve Gowda** | Janata Dal | United Front government |
| **21 Apr 1997 – 19 Mar 1998** | **I. K. Gujral** | Janata Dal | United Front government |
| **19 Mar 1998 – 22 May 2004** | **Atal Bihari Vajpayee** | BJP | NDA government |
| **22 May 2004 – 26 May 2014** | **Manmohan Singh** | Congress | UPA government; two terms |
| **26 May 2014 – Present** | **Narendra Modi** | BJP | Current PM; third consecutive term |

## 15. Who were India's 15 Prime Ministers?

The 15 individuals who have served as Prime Minister are:

1. **Jawaharlal Nehru** — 1947–1964
2. **Gulzarilal Nanda** — 1964, 1966 *(interim)*
3. **Lal Bahadur Shastri** — 1964–1966
4. **Indira Gandhi** — 1966–1977 and 1980–1984
5. **Morarji Desai** — 1977–1979
6. **Charan Singh** — 1979–1980
7. **Rajiv Gandhi** — 1984–1989
8. **V. P. Singh** — 1989–1990
9. **Chandra Shekhar** — 1990–1991
10. **P. V. Narasimha Rao** — 1991–1996
11. **Atal Bihari Vajpayee** — 1996 and 1998–2004
12. **H. D. Deve Gowda** — 1996–1997
13. **I. K. Gujral** — 1997–1998
14. **Manmohan Singh** — 2004–2014
15. **Narendra Modi** — 2014–present

**Important:** Gulzarilal Nanda served twice as **interim Prime Minister**, while Indira Gandhi and Atal Bihari Vajpayee had multiple separate periods in office.
