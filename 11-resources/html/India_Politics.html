How to read this reference

Facts, official records, political claims and historical interpretations are separated. A government’s stated objective is not automatically proof of its outcome. For every Prime Minister and major policy, the reference records documented initiatives alongside criticisms, legal findings or contested interpretations where relevant.

Political neutrality: no party or leader is assigned an overall score or winner label. Compare evidence, time period, institutional context and measurable outcomes.

LEVEL 1 · ELECTION AT A GLANCE

How a Lok Sabha election works

A simple overview before moving into the full election lifecycle.

The Indian general election in one view

1
Constituencies and voters

India is divided into 543 Lok Sabha constituencies. Eligible citizens aged 18 or above can register and vote, subject to electoral-roll rules.

↓
2
Political parties nominate candidates

Parties select candidates internally; independent candidates may also contest. The Election Commission publishes the election schedule and enforces election law and the Model Code of Conduct.

↓
3
Campaign and polling

Campaigning is regulated by law and the Model Code. Voters cast one vote for a Lok Sabha candidate in their constituency, normally using an EVM with VVPAT.

↓
4
Counting and results

Votes are counted constituency by constituency. The candidate with the highest valid vote total wins that seat (first-past-the-post); a nationwide majority of votes is not required.

↓
5
Government formation

The President appoints as Prime Minister the person who can command the confidence of the Lok Sabha. The Council of Ministers is collectively responsible to that House.

Key distinction

Indian voters do not directly elect the Prime Minister. They elect their constituency’s Member of Parliament. The party or coalition able to demonstrate Lok Sabha confidence forms the Union government.

LEVEL 2 · FULL ELECTION FLOW

Detailed Lok Sabha election lifecycle

From election notification to government formation, including the actors and legal checkpoints.

Full Lok Sabha election lifecycle

  1. Constitutional term: Lok Sabha normally continues for five years from its first meeting unless dissolved earlier. A general election follows dissolution or expiry.
  2. Election schedule: ECI announces phases, nomination dates, scrutiny, withdrawal, polling and counting.
  3. Electoral rolls: voter registration, claims and objections, photo electoral rolls and voter identification arrangements.
  4. Candidate nomination: nomination papers, security deposit, scrutiny, withdrawal and final candidate list.
  5. Campaign period: public meetings, manifestos, media, expenditure monitoring, silence period and MCC compliance.
  6. Polling: polling parties, mock poll, EVM control unit/ballot unit, VVPAT, indelible ink, secrecy and accessibility.
  7. Post-poll custody: strong-room security, candidate/agent access and documented chain of custody.
  8. Counting: EVM counting, postal ballots, round-wise tabulation and VVPAT verification under ECI instructions.
  9. Result declaration: Returning Officer declares constituency result; ECI compiles national outcome.
  10. Government formation: President appoints Prime Minister; confidence of Lok Sabha is the governing test.

LEVEL 3 · DETAILED MECHANICS

How the election machinery actually works

The constitutional, statutory, administrative and procedural layers behind Indian elections.

Detailed mechanics and institutions

Constitutional and statutory framework

  • Constitution: Articles 324–329 (elections), 326 (adult suffrage), 79–122 (Parliament), 52–78 (Union executive), and 168–212 (State legislatures).
  • Representation of the People Act, 1950: allocation of seats and electoral rolls.
  • Representation of the People Act, 1951: conduct of elections, qualifications, disqualifications, corrupt practices and election disputes.
  • Conduct of Elections Rules, 1961: procedural rules including voting and counting.
  • Election Symbols (Reservation and Allotment) Order, 1968: party recognition and symbols.
  • Model Code of Conduct: ECI’s framework for campaign conduct; it is not itself a standalone Act of Parliament.

Who does what?

  • Election Commission of India: constitutional superintendence, direction and control over elections to Parliament, State legislatures, President and Vice-President.
  • Chief Electoral Officer: state-level coordination under ECI.
  • District Election Officer: district election administration.
  • Returning Officer: nomination, scrutiny, polling/counting supervision and result declaration for a constituency.
  • Presiding Officer: manages a polling station.
  • State Election Commission: conducts Panchayat and Municipal elections under Articles 243K and 243ZA; separate from ECI.

Counting, verification and disputes

Counting is conducted under statutory rules and ECI instructions. Candidates and authorised agents can observe prescribed processes. Election petitions are adjudicated by the High Courts under the Representation of the People Act, 1951, with appeals as provided by law. Do not treat an allegation, a pending petition, a recount request or a statistical claim as a final judicial finding.

VOTING · POLLING · VERIFICATION

Voter eligibility, EVMs, VVPAT & counting

How an elector gets registered, casts a ballot and how the result is recorded and verified.

Who can vote?

  • Indian citizen, ordinarily resident in the constituency, at least 18 years old on the qualifying date, and enrolled in the electoral roll.
  • Being 18 does not automatically place a person on the roll; registration is required.
  • Overseas electors may register under the relevant provisions but generally vote in person in India.
  • Service voters, proxy voting and postal ballot facilities operate under specific eligibility and procedural rules.

EVM + VVPAT: functional flow

1
Ballot Unit

Voter presses the button next to the chosen candidate and symbol.

↓
2
Control Unit

Registers the vote after the Presiding Officer enables the ballot; prevents multiple votes in one activation.

↓
3
VVPAT slip

A printed slip is visible through a window for the prescribed period, then drops into a sealed box.

↓
4
Counting and prescribed verification

Control Unit totals are counted; VVPAT slips are verified in the manner directed by law and ECI instructions, including additional verification where ordered by courts.

Safeguards and limits

Safeguards include candidate/agent presence at specified stages, mock polls, sealing, documented custody, strong-room security, randomisation and VVPAT verification. Any claim of malfunction, manipulation or procedural breach should be evaluated against specific records, technical evidence, ECI instructions and court findings—not assumed from a viral post.

GOVERNMENT STRUCTURE · UNION

Union government · Executive, legislature & judiciary

India follows a parliamentary system. Executive authority is exercised in the constitutional framework by the President and the Council of Ministers headed by the Prime Minister.

Union government architecture

C
Constitution of India

Supreme law; distributes powers, establishes institutions and protects rights.

↓
1
Union Executive

President (constitutional head) · Prime Minister · Council of Ministers · Cabinet · ministries and departments.

2
Union Legislature · Parliament

President + Lok Sabha + Rajya Sabha; makes laws, authorises taxation and expenditure, and scrutinises government.

3
Union Judiciary

Supreme Court · High Courts · subordinate courts; interprets law and reviews constitutionality.

F
Federal distribution

Union, State and Concurrent Lists allocate legislative fields; local self-government is constitutionally recognised.

Parliamentary accountability

The Council of Ministers is collectively responsible to the Lok Sabha. The Prime Minister and ministers ordinarily remain in office while they retain the confidence of the Lok Sabha. The President generally acts on the aid and advice of the Council of Ministers, subject to constitutional provisions and limited areas of discretion.

Three organs and checks

  • Legislature: makes laws, approves budgets, questions ministers and conducts committee scrutiny.
  • Executive: implements laws, administers departments and formulates policy.
  • Judiciary: adjudicates disputes, interprets the Constitution and can invalidate unconstitutional laws or executive action.
LEGISLATURE · BICAMERAL PARLIAMENT

Parliament · Lok Sabha & Rajya Sabha

Understand membership, election method, terms, legislative procedure, money bills, committees and accountability.

The two Houses

Lok Sabha · House of the People

Directly elected from territorial constituencies. Normal term: five years unless dissolved earlier. The Council of Ministers is collectively responsible to this House.

Rajya Sabha · Council of States

Permanent House; not subject to dissolution. Members generally serve six-year terms, with roughly one-third retiring every two years. Most are elected by elected MLAs; 12 are nominated by the President for special knowledge or practical experience in literature, science, art and social service.

How a bill becomes law

  1. Bill introduced in either House, except Money Bills which can only be introduced in Lok Sabha.
  2. First reading and publication/introduction.
  3. Second reading: general discussion; possible committee referral; clause-by-clause consideration.
  4. Third reading and vote in the originating House.
  5. Other House considers the bill; ordinary bills generally require agreement by both Houses.
  6. In case of a qualifying deadlock on an ordinary bill, a joint sitting may be summoned under Article 108.
  7. President’s assent; the bill becomes an Act, subject to constitutional provisions.

Money Bills have a special procedure: Rajya Sabha may make recommendations within 14 days, but Lok Sabha may accept or reject them.

Oversight mechanisms

  • Question Hour and Zero Hour.
  • Department-related Standing Committees and other parliamentary committees.
  • Budget discussion, demands for grants and cut motions.
  • No-confidence motion and confidence motions in Lok Sabha.
  • Comptroller and Auditor General reports examined by committees including the Public Accounts Committee.
FEDERALISM · STATE POLITICS

State & Union Territory government

How state governments are formed, what Governors and Chief Ministers do, and how UT administration differs.

State government structure

  • Governor: constitutional head of a State, appointed by the President under Article 155.
  • Chief Minister: head of the elected State government; appointed by the Governor and ordinarily the leader able to command Assembly confidence.
  • Council of Ministers: aids and advises the Governor; collectively responsible to the Legislative Assembly.
  • Vidhan Sabha: directly elected lower/only House of a State legislature.
  • Vidhan Parishad: upper House in six States: Andhra Pradesh, Telangana, Bihar, Karnataka, Maharashtra and Uttar Pradesh (subject to current constitutional status).
  • High Court: constitutional court for the State or group of States/UTs.

Union Territories

UTs are administered by the President through an Administrator or Lieutenant Governor under Article 239 and the relevant law. Delhi, Puducherry and Jammu & Kashmir have legislatures with special constitutional/statutory arrangements; their powers and the role of the Lieutenant Governor are not identical.

Local self-government

The 73rd and 74th Constitutional Amendments recognise Panchayats and Municipalities. State Election Commissions conduct local body elections; the Election Commission of India conducts elections to Parliament, State legislatures, President and Vice-President.

India’s multi-party system

India uses a first-past-the-post system for Lok Sabha and most Assembly constituencies. A party can win a large seat share without winning a majority of all votes nationwide. Regional parties can be decisive in particular States and in coalition governments. Party recognition and symbols are governed by ECI rules.

National parties and regional parties

National-party recognition is based on criteria in the Election Symbols (Reservation and Allotment) Order, 1968, including vote share and/or seat performance across States. State-party recognition is separately determined. Status can change; the ECI’s current recognition order should be treated as authoritative.

Government, coalition and opposition

  • Single-party majority: one party has more than half of the effective House strength needed for confidence.
  • Coalition: two or more parties support a government through a formal or informal arrangement.
  • Outside support: a party supports confidence/supply without necessarily joining the Council of Ministers.
  • Opposition: scrutinises policy, questions ministers, offers alternatives and may lead committees; recognition as Leader of Opposition follows applicable rules and conventions.
  • Anti-defection law: Tenth Schedule provides disqualification rules, with Speaker/Chairman adjudication subject to judicial review.

How to compare party performance

Use comparable time periods and measurable indicators: manifesto commitments, legislation passed, budget allocations, implementation, audit findings, court judgments, outcomes and independent datasets. Separate a party’s national record from State-government records and distinguish party decisions from actions by individual officeholders.

CONSTITUTIONAL HISTORY · PRESIDENT OF INDIA

All Presidents of India · 1950–2026

Chronological reference to the heads of state, their terms and key constitutional context.

President’s constitutional position

The President is the constitutional head of the Union. The President is elected indirectly by an electoral college comprising elected MPs of both Houses and elected MLAs of States and specified Union Territories. The President generally acts on the aid and advice of the Council of Ministers, subject to constitutional provisions. The President is not the day-to-day head of government; that role belongs to the Prime Minister and Council of Ministers.

PEOPLE · REPRESENTATION · FEDERALISM

Population, representation & federal map

How population, constituency boundaries, language and federal design relate to political representation.

Population and delimitation

Lok Sabha constituencies are territorial and elect one MP each. The current 543 elected-seat framework reflects the constitutional and statutory arrangements following the 1971 Census freeze and later extensions. The Constitution provides for readjustment after the first census taken after 2026, subject to the relevant constitutional process. Do not assume that the 2027 census automatically changes seat allocation immediately.

Representation across States

Unlike the US Senate, Rajya Sabha representation is not equal by State. Seat allocation broadly reflects population, subject to constitutional provisions. Lok Sabha seats are also distributed among States and UTs. Delimitation determines constituency boundaries; the Delimitation Commission operates under parliamentary law.

TermMeaning
