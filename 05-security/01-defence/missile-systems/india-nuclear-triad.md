

# India Nuclear Triad

## Overview

A Nuclear Triad is the capability to deliver nuclear weapons through three independent platforms:

- Land-based missiles
- Air-based delivery systems
- Sea-based delivery systems

India's Nuclear Triad provides a credible second-strike capability and forms a central pillar of the country's nuclear deterrence strategy.

## Strategic Significance

A nuclear triad is important because it:

- Improves survivability of nuclear forces
- Ensures second-strike capability
- Strengthens deterrence
- Reduces vulnerability to a first strike
- Enhances strategic stability

The existence of multiple delivery platforms makes it difficult for an adversary to neutralize nuclear forces completely.

## India's Nuclear Doctrine

India's nuclear policy is based on:

- Credible Minimum Deterrence
- No First Use (NFU)
- Massive retaliation in response to a nuclear attack

The nuclear triad supports these principles by ensuring survivable retaliatory capabilities.

## Components of India's Nuclear Triad

### Land-Based Component

The land-based leg consists primarily of ballistic missiles.

Major systems include:

- Agni-I
- Agni-II
- Agni-III
- Agni-IV
- Agni-V

Role:

- Strategic deterrence
- Long-range nuclear delivery
- Rapid response capability

## Air-Based Component

The air-based leg consists of aircraft capable of delivering nuclear weapons.

Important platforms include:

- Mirage 2000
- Jaguar
- Rafale
- Other designated aircraft as required

Advantages:

- Operational flexibility
- Recall capability before weapon release
- Visible deterrence during crises

## Sea-Based Component

The sea-based leg is considered the most survivable component of the triad.

It is centred on nuclear-powered ballistic missile submarines (SSBNs).

Major platforms include:

- INS Arihant
- INS Arighaat
- Future Arihant-class and successor SSBNs

Associated missile systems include:

- K-15 Sagarika
- K-4 Submarine-Launched Ballistic Missile

Advantages:

- Stealth and survivability
- Continuous deterrence patrols
- Assured second-strike capability

## Completion of the Triad

India is generally considered to have completed its nuclear triad after the operational deployment of INS Arihant and successful demonstration of sea-based nuclear deterrent capabilities.

This marked a major milestone in India's strategic defence posture.

## Nuclear Command Structure

### Nuclear Command Authority (NCA)

India's nuclear forces operate under the Nuclear Command Authority.

Responsibilities include:

- Nuclear policy oversight
- Command and control
- Authorization procedures
- Strategic decision-making

### Strategic Forces Command (SFC)

The Strategic Forces Command manages operational aspects of India's strategic nuclear forces.

## Challenges

Important challenges include:

- Maintaining credible deterrence
- Technological modernization
- Secure command and control systems
- Submarine fleet expansion
- Missile development and survivability

## Future Roadmap

Future priorities include:

- Expansion of SSBN capabilities
- Development of longer-range missile systems
- Improved command and control infrastructure
- Strengthening sea-based deterrence
- Continued modernization of delivery platforms
