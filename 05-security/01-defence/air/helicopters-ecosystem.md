# Indian Helicopter Ecosystem

## Overview

India operates one of the world's largest military helicopter fleets. Helicopters play an important role in troop transport, logistics, reconnaissance, search and rescue, disaster relief, special operations, and combat missions.

The Indian helicopter ecosystem consists of indigenous platforms, licensed production programmes, and imported helicopters operated by the Indian Air Force, Indian Army, Indian Navy, Coast Guard, and other government agencies.

## Strategic Importance

Helicopters are essential because they:

- Operate in mountainous terrain
- Support border management
- Enable rapid troop deployment
- Conduct casualty evacuation
- Support disaster relief operations
- Perform maritime missions
- Enhance battlefield mobility

They are particularly important in the Himalayas, deserts, islands, and remote regions.

## Major Categories

### Utility Helicopters

Used for:

- Troop transport
- Logistics support
- Casualty evacuation
- Communication missions

Examples:

- HAL Dhruv
- Chetak
- Cheetah
- LUH

### Attack Helicopters

Used for:

- Anti-armour missions
- Close air support
- Battlefield support
- Armed reconnaissance

Examples:

- HAL Prachand (LCH)
- AH-64E Apache

### Heavy-Lift Helicopters

Used for:

- Heavy cargo transport
- Equipment movement
- Disaster response

Examples:

- CH-47F Chinook

### Naval Helicopters

Used for:

- Anti-submarine warfare
- Anti-surface warfare
- Maritime surveillance
- Search and rescue

Examples:

- MH-60R Seahawk
- ALH Dhruv Naval Variant

## Indigenous Development

### Hindustan Aeronautics Limited (HAL)

HAL is India's primary helicopter manufacturer.

Major programmes include:

- Advanced Light Helicopter (Dhruv)
- Light Combat Helicopter (Prachand)
- Light Utility Helicopter (LUH)
- Indian Multi Role Helicopter (under development)

### Advanced Light Helicopter (Dhruv)

Dhruv is India's first major indigenous multi-role helicopter programme.

Roles include:

- Transport
- Utility missions
- Search and rescue
- Medical evacuation

### Light Combat Helicopter (Prachand)

Prachand is designed for:

- High-altitude warfare
- Armed reconnaissance
- Close air support

It is particularly suited for operations in mountainous regions.

### Light Utility Helicopter (LUH)

LUH is intended to replace ageing Cheetah and Chetak helicopters.

Its primary role is utility and reconnaissance operations in difficult terrain.

## Challenges

Key challenges include:

- Replacement of ageing fleets
- High-altitude operational requirements
- Maintenance and logistics support
- Indigenous technology development
- Engine and component self-reliance

## Future Roadmap

Important future developments include:

- Expansion of Prachand induction
- Wider deployment of LUH
- Development of Indian Multi Role Helicopter (IMRH)
- Greater indigenous manufacturing
- Increased defence exports
