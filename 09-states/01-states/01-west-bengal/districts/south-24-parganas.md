# South 24 Parganas District

## District Significance

South 24 Parganas is one of the most geographically, environmentally, and strategically important districts of West Bengal.

The district is home to the Sundarbans, the world's largest mangrove forest, a UNESCO World Heritage Site, and the habitat of the Royal Bengal Tiger.

It is also known for Gangasagar, coastal tourism, fisheries, agriculture, and its critical role in climate resilience and biodiversity conservation.

## Geography

South 24 Parganas occupies the southern part of the Ganga-Brahmaputra Delta.

Major geographical features include:

- Deltaic islands
- Mangrove forests
- River channels
- Coastal regions
- Estuaries

The district contains some of the most environmentally sensitive regions in India.

## Ganga-Brahmaputra Delta

The district forms part of the world's largest delta system.

Importance:

- Fertile land
- Rich biodiversity
- Fisheries resources
- Unique ecosystems

## Sundarbans

The Sundarbans is the most famous geographical feature of the district.

Known for:

- Mangrove forests
- Royal Bengal Tiger
- Biodiversity
- River networks

It is one of the most important ecological regions in the world.

## Sundarbans National Park

A protected area within the Sundarbans.

Importance:

- Wildlife conservation
- Tiger habitat
- Eco-tourism
- Scientific research

## Tiger Reserve

The Sundarbans Tiger Reserve protects the famous Royal Bengal Tiger population.

The region is globally recognized for tiger conservation efforts.

## Biosphere Reserve

The Sundarbans Biosphere Reserve supports:

- Biodiversity conservation
- Sustainable development
- Ecological research

## UNESCO World Heritage Site

The Sundarbans is recognized as a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

Importance:

- International recognition
- Conservation support
- Tourism promotion

## Royal Bengal Tiger

The Royal Bengal Tiger is the most iconic species of the Sundarbans.

The region contains one of the world's most unique tiger habitats.

## Climate Change Challenges

The district is highly vulnerable to climate change.

Major concerns include:

- Sea-level rise
- Coastal erosion
- Salinity intrusion
- Extreme weather events

## Cyclones

The district is frequently affected by cyclones originating in the Bay of Bengal.

Challenges include:

- Flooding
- Infrastructure damage
- Agricultural losses

## Human-Wildlife Conflict

In Sundarbans regions, interactions between humans and wildlife remain a major challenge.

Conservation efforts focus on reducing conflict while protecting biodiversity.
