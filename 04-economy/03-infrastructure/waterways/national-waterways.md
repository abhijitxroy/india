# National Waterways

## Overview

National Waterways are inland water transport routes declared by the Government of India for the movement of passengers and cargo. They form an important part of India's transportation infrastructure and provide a cost-effective, fuel-efficient, and environmentally friendly mode of transport.

India has an extensive network of rivers, canals, backwaters, and creeks that can support inland navigation.

## Importance of Inland Water Transport

National Waterways help:

- Reduce logistics costs
- Improve cargo movement efficiency
- Lower fuel consumption
- Reduce road congestion
- Decrease environmental impact
- Support regional economic development
- Improve multimodal transportation networks

## Legal Framework

The National Waterways Act, 2016 expanded India's inland waterway network significantly.

Under the Act:

- 111 waterways were declared National Waterways.
- Earlier National Waterways were incorporated into a unified framework.
- Development responsibility was strengthened at the national level.

## Inland Waterways Authority of India (IWAI)

### Overview

The Inland Waterways Authority of India (IWAI) is the primary agency responsible for the development and regulation of National Waterways.

### Functions

IWAI is responsible for:

- Waterway development
- Navigation infrastructure
- Terminal construction
- Channel maintenance
- Promotion of inland water transport
- Capacity enhancement projects

## Major National Waterways

### National Waterway 1 (NW-1)

Route:

- Ganga-Bhagirathi-Hooghly River System
- Prayagraj to Haldia

Significance:

- India's longest National Waterway
- Backbone of the Jal Marg Vikas Project

### National Waterway 2 (NW-2)

Route:

- Brahmaputra River
- Dhubri to Sadiya

Importance:

- Critical for Northeast India
- Supports regional connectivity and trade

### National Waterway 3 (NW-3)

Route:

- West Coast Canal in Kerala

Importance:

- Supports passenger and cargo transport
- Connects important economic centres in Kerala

## Jal Marg Vikas Project (JMVP)

### Objective

The Jal Marg Vikas Project aims to enhance navigation capacity on National Waterway 1.

### Key Components

- Multimodal terminals
- River information systems
- Navigation aids
- Channel development
- Modern cargo handling infrastructure

## Economic Benefits

### Cost Efficiency

Water transport is generally cheaper than road and rail transport for bulk cargo.

### Industrial Development

National Waterways support:

- Manufacturing
- Agriculture
- Mining
- Export-import activities

### Regional Connectivity

Waterways improve access to remote and underserved regions.

## Challenges

Major challenges include:

- Seasonal water level variations
- River sedimentation
- Navigation constraints
- Infrastructure gaps
- Limited cargo volumes in some routes
- Environmental considerations

## Future Roadmap

Key priorities include:

- Expansion of cargo movement
- Development of multimodal logistics hubs
- Better integration with road and rail networks
- Modern navigation systems
- Increased private sector participation
