# Jainism in India

## Overview

Jainism is one of the oldest religions originating in India.

It emphasizes non-violence, truth, self-discipline, and spiritual liberation through ethical living.

Jainism has made significant contributions to Indian philosophy, culture, literature, art, and architecture.

## Origin

Jain tradition traces its origins through a succession of spiritual teachers known as Tirthankaras.

### Number of Tirthankaras

- 24

### First Tirthankara

- Rishabhanatha (Adinatha)

### Twenty-Fourth Tirthankara

- Mahavira

## Mahavira

### Birth

- 6th Century BCE

### Importance

Mahavira is regarded as the last Tirthankara and the most important historical figure associated with Jainism.

He organized and spread Jain teachings across northern India.

## Core Principles

### Ahimsa

- Non-violence toward all living beings

### Satya

- Truthfulness

### Asteya

- Non-stealing

### Brahmacharya

- Celibacy or self-control

### Aparigraha

- Non-possession and detachment

## Three Jewels (Triratna)

The path to liberation is based on:

- Right Faith
- Right Knowledge
- Right Conduct

## Sects of Jainism

### Digambara

- Monastic tradition emphasizes complete renunciation

### Svetambara

- Monks and nuns wear white robes

Both traditions share the core teachings of Jainism.

## Scriptures

Important Jain religious texts include:

- Agamas
- Kalpa Sutra
- Tattvartha Sutra

## Jainism and Indian Society

Jainism contributed to:

- Non-violence traditions
- Ethical philosophy
- Trade and commerce
- Education and learning
- Charity and social welfare

## Important Jain Pilgrimage Sites

### Shikharji

- Jharkhand

### Palitana

- Gujarat

### Shravanabelagola

- Karnataka

### Ranakpur

- Rajasthan

### Dilwara Temples

- Rajasthan

## Art and Architecture

Jain architecture is known for:

- Intricate stone carvings
- Marble temples
- Cave monuments
- Monumental statues

The statue of Bahubali at Shravanabelagola is among the most famous Jain monuments.

## Distribution in India

Major Jain populations are found in:

- Rajasthan
- Gujarat
- Maharashtra
- Karnataka
- Madhya Pradesh

## Significance

Jainism remains an important part of India's religious and philosophical heritage.

Its emphasis on non-violence and ethical conduct continues to influence Indian thought and society.
