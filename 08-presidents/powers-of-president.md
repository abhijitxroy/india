# Powers of the President of India

## Overview

The President of India is the constitutional Head of State.

Most presidential powers are exercised on the aid and advice of the Council of Ministers headed by the Prime Minister. However, the President remains a key constitutional authority and plays an important role in maintaining constitutional governance.

## Importance

- Constitutional stability
- Democratic continuity
- Legislative functioning
- Executive administration
- National security
- Emergency governance

## Executive Powers

The President:

- Appoints the Prime Minister
- Appoints Union Ministers
- Appoints Governors
- Appoints the Attorney General of India
- Appoints the Comptroller and Auditor General
- Appoints Supreme Court and High Court Judges
- Appoints Election Commissioners and other constitutional authorities

All executive actions of the Union Government are carried out in the name of the President.

## Legislative Powers

The President:

- Summons Parliament
- Prorogues Parliament
- Dissolves the Lok Sabha
- Addresses Parliament
- Nominates members where constitutionally permitted
- Gives assent to Bills
- Returns ordinary bills for reconsideration
- Promulgates Ordinances when Parliament is not in session

## Financial Powers

The President:

- Causes the Union Budget to be presented in Parliament
- Recommends Money Bills before introduction
- Recommends financial legislation where required
- Oversees operation of the Contingency Fund of India

## Judicial Powers

The President can grant:

- Pardon
- Reprieve
- Respite
- Remission
- Suspension
- Commutation

These powers are exercised under Article 72.

## Diplomatic Powers

The President:

- Represents India internationally
- Appoints ambassadors and high commissioners
- Receives foreign diplomats
- Signs international agreements and treaties on behalf of India

## Military Powers

The President is the Supreme Commander of the Armed Forces.

The President:

- Appoints service chiefs
- Exercises supreme command of the Army, Navy, and Air Force
- Declares war and peace subject to parliamentary approval and constitutional procedures

## Emergency Powers

### National Emergency

- Article 352
- War, external aggression, or armed rebellion

### President's Rule

- Article 356
- Failure of constitutional machinery in a state

### Financial Emergency

- Article 360
- Threat to India's financial stability

## Limits on Presidential Powers

- India follows a Parliamentary System.
- The President normally acts on ministerial advice.
- Real executive authority rests with the Council of Ministers.
- The President cannot govern independently of the Constitution.
