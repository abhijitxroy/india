# Anti-Defection Law

## Overview

The Anti-Defection Law was introduced to reduce political defections and promote stability in elected governments.

It seeks to discourage elected representatives from changing political allegiance in ways that may affect the mandate given by voters.

The law is one of the most important political reforms in India's parliamentary democracy.

---

## Why Study the Anti-Defection Law?

Studying the Anti-Defection Law helps understand:

- Parliamentary democracy
- Political stability
- Party systems
- Legislative governance
- Coalition politics
- Constitutional reforms

It is central to understanding how elected governments maintain stability.

---

```text
Frequent Political Defections
            ↓
```

The law emerged in response to concerns about frequent party switching by elected representatives.

## Main Objective

The law aims to:

- Reduce political defections
- Promote government stability
- Strengthen party discipline
- Protect electoral mandates

---

```text
Election
    ↓
Party Membership
    ↓
Legislative Conduct
```

The law establishes rules regarding party affiliation and legislative behavior.

---

## Key Features

### Political Stability

- Discourages frequent party switching
- Supports continuity of governments
- Reduces instability in legislatures

### Party Discipline

- Encourages adherence to party positions
- Strengthens party-based parliamentary systems

### Legislative Accountability

- Defines consequences for certain forms of defection
- Establishes procedures under the law

---

## Public Debate

### Supporters' Arguments

Common arguments include:

- Greater political stability
- Reduced opportunistic defections
- Stronger democratic mandates
- Better governance continuity

### Critics' Arguments

Common arguments include:

- Reduced legislative independence
- Stronger party control over representatives
- Limits on individual decision-making
