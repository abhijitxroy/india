# Nepal

## Overview

Nepal is one of India's closest neighbours and an important partner in South Asia.

The relationship is based on historical ties, cultural links, open borders, economic cooperation, and people-to-people connections.

Nepal occupies a significant position in India's Neighbourhood First Policy and Himalayan strategy.

## Geography

Nepal is a landlocked Himalayan country.

It shares borders with:

- India
- China

Indian states bordering Nepal include:

- Uttarakhand
- Uttar Pradesh
- Bihar
- West Bengal
- Sikkim

## Historical Background

India and Nepal share centuries-old civilizational and cultural connections.

The relationship is strengthened by:

- Shared religious heritage
- Cultural exchanges
- Family ties across the border
- Historical interactions

## Treaty of Peace and Friendship

### Signed

- 1950

The treaty forms an important foundation of modern India–Nepal relations.

## Open Border

India and Nepal maintain an open border.

This facilitates:

- Movement of people
- Trade and commerce
- Tourism
- Cultural exchanges

## Areas of Cooperation

### Trade and Commerce

India is Nepal's largest trading partner.

Major sectors include:

- Petroleum products
- Consumer goods
- Agriculture
- Manufacturing

### Connectivity

Cooperation includes:

- Road links
- Rail connectivity
- Energy transmission lines
- Border infrastructure

### Energy Cooperation

Major areas include:

- Hydropower development
- Electricity trade
- Energy infrastructure projects

### Development Partnership

India supports projects related to:

- Education
- Healthcare
- Infrastructure
- Community development

## Cultural Relations

Shared cultural links include:

- Hindu and Buddhist heritage
- Pilgrimage circuits
- Educational exchanges
- Tourism

## Regional Cooperation

India and Nepal cooperate through:

- SAARC
- BIMSTEC
- BBIN

## Importance for India

Nepal is important because of:

- Himalayan security
- Water resources
- Connectivity to the Himalayas
- Cultural ties
- Neighbourhood First Policy

## Challenges

Major challenges include:

- Border-related issues
- Political sensitivities
- Infrastructure constraints
- Trade and transit concerns

Despite these challenges, bilateral relations remain extensive and important.

## Significance

Nepal is one of India's most important neighbours and plays a major role in regional stability, connectivity, and Himalayan geopolitics.
