# Pakistan

## Overview

Pakistan is one of India's most important neighbours and a key factor in India's foreign policy, security, and regional strategy.

The relationship has been shaped by historical developments, territorial disputes, security concerns, trade issues, and people-to-people connections.

## Geography

Pakistan shares borders with:

- India
- Afghanistan
- Iran
- China

Indian states and union territories bordering Pakistan include:

- Jammu and Kashmir
- Ladakh
- Punjab
- Rajasthan
- Gujarat

## Historical Background

### Partition of India

- 1947

British India was partitioned into India and Pakistan.

The partition resulted in large-scale migration, violence, and long-term geopolitical challenges.

## Major Issues in Bilateral Relations

### Jammu and Kashmir

The Jammu and Kashmir issue remains one of the most significant areas of disagreement between the two countries.

### Cross-Border Terrorism

India has consistently raised concerns regarding cross-border terrorism.

### Border Security

Security management along the International Border and Line of Control remains important.

### Trade Restrictions

Economic relations have been affected by political and security concerns.

## Wars Between India and Pakistan

### First India–Pakistan War

- 1947–48

### Second India–Pakistan War

- 1965

### Bangladesh Liberation War

- 1971

### Kargil Conflict

- 1999

## Important Agreements

### Tashkent Agreement

- 1966

### Simla Agreement

- 1972

### Lahore Declaration

- 1999

These agreements aimed to improve bilateral relations and reduce tensions.

## Confidence Building Measures

Important measures have included:

- Diplomatic dialogue
- Cultural exchanges
- Sports interactions
- Transport links

## Trade Relations

Potential areas of trade include:

- Agriculture
- Textiles
- Pharmaceuticals
- Consumer goods

Trade levels have fluctuated because of political developments.

## Regional Cooperation

India and Pakistan are members of:

- SAARC

Regional cooperation has often been affected by bilateral tensions.

## Nuclear Dimension

Both India and Pakistan are nuclear-armed states.

Strategic stability and crisis management remain important aspects of regional security.

## Importance for India

Pakistan is important for:

- National security
- Border management
- Counter-terrorism efforts
- Regional stability
- South Asian geopolitics

## Challenges

Major challenges include:

- Terrorism concerns
- Border tensions
- Political mistrust
- Limited economic engagement

## Significance

India–Pakistan relations remain one of the most important and complex relationships in South Asia and continue to influence regional security and diplomacy.
