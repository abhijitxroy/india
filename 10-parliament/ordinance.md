
# Ordinance in India

## Overview

An Ordinance is a temporary law issued by the President of India when Parliament is not in session and immediate legislative action is considered necessary.

The Ordinance-making power allows the government to respond to urgent situations without waiting for Parliament to reconvene.

---

## Why Study Ordinances?

Studying Ordinances helps understand:

- Emergency legislative action
- Constitutional powers of the President
- Executive-legislative relationship
- Temporary law-making mechanisms
- Parliamentary oversight

---

## Constitutional Basis

The Ordinance-making power is provided under Article 123 of the Constitution of India.

The President exercises this power on the aid and advice of the Council of Ministers.

---

## When Can an Ordinance Be Issued?

```text
Parliament Not In Session
            ↓
Immediate Action Needed
            ↓
Government Recommendation
            ↓
President Issues Ordinance
```

Both Houses of Parliament must not be in session at the time of issuance.

---

## Ordinance Process

```text
Policy Need
      ↓
Government Recommendation
      ↓
President
      ↓
Ordinance Issued
      ↓
Temporary Law
      ↓
Parliament Review
```

---

## Legal Effect

An Ordinance has the same force and effect as an Act of Parliament while it remains in operation.

However, it is temporary in nature.

---

## Parliamentary Approval

After Parliament reconvenes:

```text
Ordinance
      ↓
Placed Before Parliament
      ↓
Approved
      ↓
Law Continues

OR

Rejected / Not Passed
      ↓
Ordinance Ceases
```

Parliament ultimately decides whether the provisions should continue.

---

## Why Ordinances Are Used

Common reasons include:

- Urgent public matters
- Economic measures
- Administrative requirements
- Legal gaps requiring immediate action

---

## Ordinance vs Act

| Ordinance | Act |
|----------|----------|
| Temporary law | Permanent law (until amended/repealed) |
| Issued when Parliament is not in session | Passed through Parliament |
| Issued by President on advice of government | Passed by Parliament and assented to by President |
| Must later be considered by Parliament | Already completed legislative process |

---

## Checks and Balances

The Ordinance power is not intended to replace normal law-making.

Parliament retains the authority to approve, modify, or reject measures introduced through Ordinances.

---

## Quick Revision

- Ordinance is a temporary law.
- Issued under Article 123.
- Used when Parliament is not in session.
- Issued by the President on government advice.
- Has the force of law while in operation.
- Must be placed before Parliament after it reconvenes.
