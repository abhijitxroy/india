# Delhi Sultanate

## What Was the Delhi Sultanate?

The Delhi Sultanate was a series of Muslim dynasties that ruled large parts of the Indian subcontinent from 1206 CE to 1526 CE.

It was one of the most important periods in medieval Indian history and served as a bridge between the early medieval kingdoms and the Mughal Empire.

The capital was primarily Delhi, which became an important political and administrative center.

## What Existed Before It?

Before the Delhi Sultanate:

- Northern India was ruled by several Rajput kingdoms.
- Regional kingdoms controlled different parts of the subcontinent.
- Political power was fragmented.

The defeat of Prithviraj Chauhan by Muhammad Ghori in the late 12th century paved the way for the establishment of the Delhi Sultanate.

---

## How Did It Begin?

After Muhammad Ghori's death, his general Qutb-ud-din Aibak established independent rule in Delhi in 1206 CE.

This marked the beginning of the Delhi Sultanate.

---

## Major Dynasties

### Mamluk (Slave) Dynasty

Important rulers:

- Qutb-ud-din Aibak
- Iltutmish
- Razia Sultan
- Balban

Highlights:

- Established the Sultanate's foundations.
- Strengthened administration.
- Qutub Minar construction continued during this period.

---

### Khalji Dynasty

Important rulers:

- Jalal-ud-din Khalji
- Alauddin Khalji

Highlights:

- Expansion into large parts of India.
- Successful defense against Mongol invasions.
- Market and price-control reforms.

---

### Tughlaq Dynasty

Important rulers:

- Ghiyasuddin Tughlaq
- Muhammad bin Tughlaq
- Firoz Shah Tughlaq

Highlights:

- Administrative experiments.
- Capital shift attempt from Delhi to Daulatabad.
- Large public works and irrigation projects.

---

### Sayyid Dynasty

Highlights:

- Ruled after Timur's invasion.
- Controlled a smaller territory compared to earlier dynasties.

---

### Lodi Dynasty

Important rulers:

- Bahlul Lodi
- Sikandar Lodi
- Ibrahim Lodi

Highlights:

- First Afghan dynasty of the Delhi Sultanate.
- Ended after the First Battle of Panipat in 1526.

---

## Administration

The Delhi Sultanate introduced several administrative practices:

- Centralized governance
- Revenue collection systems
- Military administration
- Provincial governance structure
- Record keeping and taxation mechanisms

Many later governments adopted and refined these systems.

---

## Cultural Impact

The period saw:

- Growth of Indo-Islamic architecture
- Development of Persian influence in administration
- Expansion of trade networks
- Cultural interaction between different communities
- Growth of literature and learning

Important monuments include:

- Qutub Minar
- Alai Darwaza
- Tughlaqabad Fort

---

## What Changed After It?

In 1526:

- Babur defeated Ibrahim Lodi in the First Battle of Panipat.
- The Mughal Empire was established.
- A new phase of Indian history began.

---

## Significance

The Delhi Sultanate:

- Unified large parts of northern India under a central authority.
- Strengthened administrative systems.
- Influenced architecture, culture, and governance.
- Prepared the foundation for the Mughal Empire.

---

## Quick Revision

- Duration: 1206–1526 CE
- Capital: Delhi

- Began with Qutb-ud-din Aibak.
- Expanded administration and governance.
