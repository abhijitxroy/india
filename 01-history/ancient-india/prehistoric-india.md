# Prehistoric India

## What Is Prehistoric India?

Prehistoric India refers to the period before the invention of writing and recorded history in the Indian subcontinent.

Since written records did not exist, historians rely on:

- Archaeological discoveries
- Stone tools
- Cave paintings
- Fossils
- Human settlements
- Burial sites

This period covers the earliest human presence in the Indian subcontinent and the gradual development of agriculture, villages, and early societies.

---

## Timeline

| Period | Approximate Timeline |
|----------|----------|
| Paleolithic Age (Old Stone Age) | c. 2.5 million BCE – 10,000 BCE |
| Mesolithic Age (Middle Stone Age) | c. 10,000 BCE – 8,000 BCE |
| Neolithic Age (New Stone Age) | c. 8,000 BCE – 3,000 BCE |
| Chalcolithic Age (Copper Age) | c. 3,000 BCE – 1,500 BCE |
| Early Urban Civilizations | After c. 3300 BCE |

---

## Why Is This Period Important?

This was the period when humans gradually moved from:

```text
Hunting
    ↓
Gathering
    ↓
Farming
    ↓
Village Life
    ↓
Civilization
```

The foundations of later Indian civilization were built during this time.

---

## Paleolithic Age (Old Stone Age)

### Characteristics

- Earliest phase of human history.
- People lived as hunter-gatherers.
- Stone tools were used for survival.
- Humans lived in caves and temporary shelters.

### Important Sites

- Bhimbetka (Madhya Pradesh)
- Hunsgi (Karnataka)
- Soan Valley (Pakistan region)

### Lifestyle

- Hunting animals
- Collecting fruits and plants
- Moving frequently in search of food

---

## Mesolithic Age (Middle Stone Age)

### Characteristics

- Smaller and more advanced stone tools appeared.
- Humans began settling for longer periods.
- Fishing became more common.
- Early domestication of animals began.

### Important Sites

- Bagor (Rajasthan)
- Langhnaj (Gujarat)
- Bhimbetka rock shelters

---

## Neolithic Age (New Stone Age)

This period brought major changes.

### Key Developments

- Agriculture developed.
- Permanent settlements emerged.
- Domestication of animals increased.
- Pottery became common.

### Important Sites

- Mehrgarh
- Burzahom
- Chirand
- Hallur

### Why It Matters

The Neolithic Revolution transformed human society from food collection to food production.

---

## Chalcolithic Age (Copper Age)

Humans began using both:

- Stone tools
- Copper tools

### Features

- Improved farming
- Larger settlements
- Trade between communities
- Development of crafts

This period acted as a bridge between village societies and urban civilizations.

---

## Cave Paintings and Art

One of the most important prehistoric discoveries in India is the rock art found at Bhimbetka.

These paintings depict:

- Hunting scenes
- Animals
- Daily life
- Social activities

They provide valuable insight into prehistoric society.

---

## Mehrgarh: A Turning Point

Mehrgarh is one of the earliest known farming settlements in South Asia.

Important features:

- Agriculture
- Animal domestication
- Permanent settlements
- Early village life

It represents the transition from prehistoric communities toward organized civilization.

---

## Transition to Civilization

Over thousands of years:

- Agriculture improved.
- Population increased.
- Trade expanded.
- Settlements grew larger.

These developments eventually led to the emergence of the Indus Valley Civilization.

---

## Significance

Prehistoric India helps explain:

- How humans first lived in the subcontinent.
- The origins of agriculture.
- Development of settlements.
- Evolution of technology.
- Foundations of later civilizations.

Without understanding prehistoric India, it is difficult to understand how the Indus Valley Civilization emerged.

---

## Quick Revision

- Prehistoric India existed before written records.
- Stone Age is divided into Paleolithic, Mesolithic, and Neolithic periods.
- Agriculture emerged during the Neolithic Age.
- Mehrgarh was one of the earliest farming settlements.
- Bhimbetka is famous for prehistoric rock paintings.
- Chalcolithic communities used both stone and copper tools.
- These developments eventually led to the Indus Valley Civilization.
