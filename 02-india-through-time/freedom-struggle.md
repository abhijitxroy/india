

# Indian Freedom Struggle

## Overview

The Indian Freedom Struggle was a long political, social, and national movement that aimed to end British rule in India.

It involved:

- Political movements
- Mass protests
- Revolutionary activities
- Social reforms
- Armed resistance
- Diplomatic efforts

The struggle lasted for many decades and eventually resulted in India's independence on 15 August 1947.

---

## Why Did the Freedom Struggle Begin?

Several factors contributed:

### Political Reasons

- Foreign rule over India.
- Limited participation of Indians in governance.
- Annexation of Indian territories.

### Economic Reasons

- Heavy taxation.
- Economic exploitation.
- Decline of traditional industries.
- Frequent famines and hardship.

### Social Reasons

- Growing political awareness.
- Expansion of education.
- Rise of newspapers and public debate.

## Revolt of 1857

Often called:

- First War of Independence (by many Indian historians)
- Revolt of 1857
- Sepoy Mutiny (common British term)

Important leaders:

- Rani Lakshmibai
- Bahadur Shah Zafar
- Nana Sahib
- Tantia Tope

Although unsuccessful, it became a major symbol of resistance.

---

## Formation of Indian National Congress (1885)

The Indian National Congress (INC) became one of the most important political organizations in India's freedom movement.

Early goals included:

- Greater political representation
- Administrative reforms
- Constitutional progress

Over time, it became a leading force for independence.

---

## Partition of Bengal (1905)

The British partitioned Bengal in 1905.

The decision triggered:

- Protests
- Boycotts
- Nationalist movements

The partition was later reversed in 1911.

This event played a major role in strengthening Indian nationalism.

---

## Swadeshi Movement

Main idea:

- Use Indian-made goods.
- Boycott foreign products.

The movement encouraged:

- Economic self-reliance
- National awareness
- Indigenous industries

---

## Jallianwala Bagh Massacre (1919)

British troops opened fire on a gathering in Amritsar.

The incident:

- Caused widespread outrage.
- Increased opposition to British rule.
- Became a turning point in the freedom movement.

---

## Mahatma Gandhi and Mass Movements

### Non-Cooperation Movement (1920–22)

Led by Mahatma Gandhi.

People were encouraged to:

- Boycott British institutions.
- Reject foreign goods.
- Participate in peaceful resistance.

---

### Civil Disobedience Movement (1930–34)

Focused on openly opposing unjust laws.

Most famous event:

### Salt March (Dandi March)

In 1930, Gandhi marched to Dandi and symbolically produced salt in protest against British salt laws.

The march became an international symbol of non-violent resistance.

---

## Revolutionary Movements

Not all freedom fighters followed non-violent methods.

Important revolutionaries included:

- Bhagat Singh
- Chandrashekhar Azad
- Ram Prasad Bismil
- Surya Sen

These groups believed armed resistance was necessary against colonial rule.

---

## Subhas Chandra Bose and INA

Subhas Chandra Bose played a unique role in the freedom struggle.

He established the:

- Indian National Army (INA)

Important slogan:

> Give me blood, and I shall give you freedom.

The INA fought alongside forces opposing British rule during World War II.

---

## Quit India Movement (1942)

Launched by the Indian National Congress.

Main demand:

- Immediate end to British rule.

The movement saw large-scale protests across India.

---

## Role of Women

Women played an important role.

Examples:

- Sarojini Naidu
- Aruna Asaf Ali
- Kasturba Gandhi
- Rani Lakshmibai (earlier resistance)

Their contributions strengthened the movement.

---

## Why Did India Gain Independence?

Several factors contributed:

- Decades of nationalist movements.
- Political pressure.
- Economic challenges faced by Britain.
- Impact of World War II.
- Growing demand for self-rule.
- International changes after the war.

---

## Independence (1947)

On 15 August 1947:

- British rule ended.
- India became independent.
- Partition created India and Pakistan.

This marked the culmination of the freedom struggle.

---

## Significance

The Freedom Struggle:

- Ended colonial rule.
- Created an independent India.
- Inspired anti-colonial movements worldwide.
- Strengthened democratic and constitutional aspirations.

---
