

# Municipal Corporation

## Overview

A Municipal Corporation is the highest form of urban local self-government in India.

It is established in large cities with substantial populations and complex administrative requirements.

Municipal Corporations are responsible for providing civic services, urban planning, infrastructure development, and local governance.

## Importance

Municipal Corporations are important because they:

- Manage large urban centres
- Deliver essential civic services
- Support urban development
- Promote local democracy
- Improve public infrastructure
- Coordinate city administration

## Constitutional Basis

Urban local bodies were strengthened through the 74th Constitutional Amendment Act, 1992.

This amendment provided constitutional status to urban local self-government institutions.

## Composition

A Municipal Corporation generally consists of:

- Mayor
- Elected councillors
- Municipal Commissioner
- Standing committees
- Administrative departments

Councillors represent individual wards within the city.

## Major Functions

### Civic Services

- Water supply
- Sanitation
- Solid waste management
- Street lighting
- Public health services

### Infrastructure Development

- Roads and bridges
- Urban transport facilities
- Drainage systems
- Public buildings
- Parks and recreational facilities

### Urban Planning

- Land-use planning
- Building approvals
- Development regulations
- City expansion planning

### Public Welfare

- Health programmes
- Educational initiatives
- Social welfare activities
- Environmental protection measures

## Sources of Revenue

Municipal Corporations receive revenue from:

- Property tax
- User charges and fees
- State government grants
- Central government grants
- Licences and permits
- Municipal bonds in some cities

## Challenges

Common challenges include:

- Rapid urbanisation
- Traffic congestion
- Waste management
- Pollution control
- Infrastructure funding
- Population growth
