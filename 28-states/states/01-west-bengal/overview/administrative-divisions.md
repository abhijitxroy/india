# Administrative Divisions of West Bengal

## State Significance

West Bengal's administrative structure enables governance, law and order, revenue collection, development planning, local self-government, and public service delivery across the state.

## District Administration

Each district is headed by a District Magistrate (DM), who is responsible for administration, revenue, elections, disaster management, and coordination of government departments.

### Key District Officials

| Position | Responsibility |
|----------|----------|
| District Magistrate (DM) | Overall district administration |
| Superintendent of Police (SP) | Law and order |
| Chief Medical Officer of Health (CMOH) | Public health administration |
| District Education Officer | Education administration |

## Urban Administration

Urban areas are governed through:

- Municipal Corporations
- Municipalities
- Nagar Panchayats

Major Municipal Corporations include Kolkata, Howrah, Asansol, Durgapur, Siliguri, Chandannagar, Bidhannagar, and Haldia.

## Rural Administration

Rural administration follows the Panchayati Raj system.

```text
Zila Parishad
    ↓
Panchayat Samiti
    ↓
Gram Panchayat
```

## Administrative Evolution

- Bengal Presidency was the primary administrative unit during British rule.
- West Bengal was formed in 1947 following the Partition of India.
- Districts have been reorganised several times to improve governance and service delivery.
- New districts such as Kalimpong, Jhargram, Alipurduar, and Paschim Bardhaman were created in recent decades.

## Quick Revision

- West Bengal has 5 administrative divisions.
- The state has 23 districts.
- Kolkata is the state capital.
- Urban governance is managed by Municipal Corporations and Municipalities.
- Rural governance follows the three-tier Panchayati Raj system.
