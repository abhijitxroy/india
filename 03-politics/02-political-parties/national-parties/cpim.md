
# Communist Party of India (Marxist) – CPI(M)

## Overview

The Communist Party of India (Marxist), commonly known as CPI(M), is one of India's major left-wing political parties.

It emerged from a split within the Communist Party of India (CPI) and became one of the most influential communist parties in India.

The party has played a significant role in national politics, state politics, labor movements, and coalition governments.

## Historical Background

### Origins

The CPI(M) was formed in 1964 following ideological and organizational differences within the Communist Party of India.

The split created two separate parties:

```text
Communist Party of India (CPI)
            ↓
      Split (1964)
            ↓
CPI                    CPI(M)
```

---

### Expansion

Following its formation, CPI(M) expanded its presence in several states.

The party became particularly influential in:

- West Bengal
- Kerala
- Tripura

## Ideological Themes

The CPI(M) is commonly associated with:

- Marxist political thought
- Workers' rights
- Farmers' rights
- Social equality
- Public welfare
- Land reforms
- Secularism

Supporters and critics may interpret these themes differently.

## Electoral Presence

The CPI(M) contests elections at multiple levels:

- Lok Sabha
- Rajya Sabha
- State Legislative Assemblies
- Municipal Elections
- Panchayat Elections

Its electoral influence varies across regions and time periods.

## Role in State Politics

The CPI(M) has played an important role in state politics.

Particularly notable are its periods in government in:

- West Bengal
- Kerala
- Tripura

The party has been associated with several long-running state governments.

## CPI(M) and Coalition Politics

The CPI(M) has frequently participated in coalition politics.

It has worked with other parties in various alliances at national and state levels.

Coalition politics has been an important part of its parliamentary strategy.
