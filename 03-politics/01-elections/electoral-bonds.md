# Electoral Bonds

## Overview

Electoral Bonds were financial instruments introduced to facilitate donations to eligible political parties through the banking system.

The scheme was presented as a mechanism to increase the use of formal banking channels in political funding.

Electoral Bonds became one of the most debated electoral finance reforms in India.

## Introduction

### Launched

- 2018

### Issuing Institution

- State Bank of India (SBI)

The bonds could be purchased by individuals, companies, and other eligible entities.

## What is an Electoral Bond?

An Electoral Bond was a bearer banking instrument.

It could be purchased from authorized branches of the State Bank of India and donated to eligible political parties.

The receiving political party could redeem the bond through its designated bank account.

## Eligibility of Political Parties

Political parties registered under the Representation of the People Act, 1951 were eligible to receive Electoral Bonds if they secured at least 1% of votes in the most recent Lok Sabha or State Assembly election.

## Objectives

The stated objectives included:

- Encouraging political donations through formal banking channels
- Reducing cash-based political funding
- Improving transparency in financial transactions
- Strengthening accountability in political finance

## Features

### Purchase Through Banking System

Electoral Bonds could only be purchased through authorized banking channels.

### Fixed Denominations

The bonds were available in multiple denominations.

### Time-Limited Validity

The bonds had a limited validity period for redemption.

### Bearer Instrument

The donor's identity was not disclosed to the receiving political party through the bond itself.

## Criticisms

Major criticisms included:

- Lack of public transparency regarding donors
- Concerns about anonymous political funding
- Potential imbalance in political competition
- Limited public visibility into donation sources

## Supreme Court Judgment

In 2024, the Supreme Court of India struck down the Electoral Bonds Scheme.

The Court held that provisions enabling anonymous political funding were unconstitutional and directed disclosure of relevant information regarding Electoral Bonds.

## Significance

The Electoral Bonds debate highlighted important issues relating to:

- Political funding
- Electoral transparency
- Right to information
- Democratic accountability
- Campaign finance reforms

## Related Laws and Institutions

Important institutions and laws connected to electoral funding include:

- Election Commission of India
- Representation of the People Act, 1951
- Income Tax Act provisions relating to political donations

## Challenges in Political Funding

Major challenges include:

- Transparency of donations
- Regulation of campaign finance
- Prevention of undue influence
- Public trust in electoral processes
