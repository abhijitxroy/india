# Netaji Subhas Chandra Bose

## Overview

Subhas Chandra Bose, popularly known as Netaji, was one of the most influential leaders of India's freedom struggle.

He advocated complete independence from British rule and became internationally known for leading the Indian National Army (INA) during the Second World War. He remains one of the most respected and admired figures in modern Indian history.

## Early Life

- Born into a prominent Bengali family.
- Excelled academically.
- Qualified for the Indian Civil Service (ICS).
- Resigned from the ICS to join the freedom movement.
- Inspired by nationalism and the struggle for complete independence.

## Political Career

### Indian National Congress

- Joined the national movement under Congress leadership.
- Emerged as a popular young nationalist leader.
- Advocated stronger action against British rule.
- Served as President of the Indian National Congress.

### Differences with Congress Leadership

- Favored a more aggressive approach toward independence.
- Developed differences with some senior Congress leaders.
- Eventually formed the Forward Bloc in 1939.

## Escape from India

In 1941, Bose escaped British surveillance and left India.

He traveled through multiple countries seeking international support for India's independence.

## Indian National Army (INA)

### Leadership

- Took command of the Indian National Army.
- Mobilized Indian prisoners of war and overseas Indians.
- Sought to liberate India from British rule through armed struggle.

### Azad Hind Government

- Established the Provisional Government of Free India (Azad Hind).
- Promoted the slogan:

> Give me blood, and I will give you freedom.

### National Slogans

Associated with:

- Jai Hind
- Delhi Chalo
- Ittehad, Itmad, Qurbani

## Contribution to the Freedom Movement

- Inspired nationalist sentiment across India.
- Expanded the international dimension of the freedom struggle.
- Strengthened the idea of complete independence.
- Influenced later developments in the final phase of British rule.

## Legacy

- One of India's most celebrated freedom fighters.
- Symbol of courage, sacrifice, and patriotism.
- INA remains an important chapter in Indian history.
- Remembered across India and particularly in Bengal.
- Numerous institutions, roads, airports, and memorials are named in his honor.
