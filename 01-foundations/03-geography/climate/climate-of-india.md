# Climate of India

## Overview

India experiences a diverse climate due to its large geographical extent, varied relief features, altitude differences, proximity to seas, and the influence of monsoon winds.

The climate of India is generally described as a tropical monsoon climate, although significant regional variations exist.

## Factors Influencing Climate

### Latitude

The Tropic of Cancer passes through the middle of India.

As a result:

- Southern India experiences tropical conditions
- Northern India experiences greater seasonal variation

### Altitude

Temperature generally decreases with increasing altitude.

Mountain regions are cooler than surrounding plains.

### Distance from the Sea

Coastal regions experience a moderate climate due to maritime influence.

Interior regions experience more extreme temperatures.

### Himalayas

The Himalayas influence India's climate by:

- Blocking cold winds from Central Asia
- Forcing monsoon winds to rise and produce rainfall
- Influencing river systems and weather patterns

### Monsoon Winds

Monsoon winds are the most important factor shaping India's climate.

They determine the distribution and timing of rainfall across the country.

## Seasons of India

The India Meteorological Department generally recognizes four major seasons.

### Winter Season

Period:

- January to February

Characteristics:

- Cool and dry weather
- Low temperatures in northern India
- Snowfall in Himalayan regions

### Summer Season

Period:

- March to May

Characteristics:

- High temperatures
- Heat waves in northern and central India
- Formation of low-pressure areas

### Southwest Monsoon Season

Period:

- June to September

Characteristics:

- Main rainy season
- Majority of annual rainfall received during this period
- Vital for agriculture and water resources

### Retreating Monsoon Season

Period:

- October to December

Characteristics:

- Withdrawal of monsoon winds
- Rainfall in parts of southern India
- Cyclonic activity over the Bay of Bengal

## Monsoon System

### Southwest Monsoon

The southwest monsoon arrives in India from the Arabian Sea and Bay of Bengal.

It provides most of the country's annual rainfall.

### Northeast Monsoon

The northeast monsoon primarily affects:

- Tamil Nadu
- Coastal Andhra Pradesh
- Parts of southeastern India

## Rainfall Distribution

Rainfall varies greatly across India.

### High Rainfall Areas

- Western Ghats
- Northeastern India
- Himalayan foothills

### Low Rainfall Areas

- Thar Desert
- Parts of western Rajasthan
- Cold desert regions of Ladakh

## Climate Regions

Major climatic regions include:

- Tropical wet climate
- Tropical wet and dry climate
- Semi-arid climate
- Arid climate
- Mountain climate

## Importance of Climate

Climate influences:

- Agriculture
- Water availability
- Biodiversity
- Human settlements
- Economic activities
- Disaster management

## Climate Challenges

Major challenges include:

- Heat waves
- Droughts
- Floods
- Cyclones
- Changing rainfall patterns
- Climate change impacts
