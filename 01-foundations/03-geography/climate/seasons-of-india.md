# Seasons of India

## Overview

India experiences distinct seasons due to variations in temperature, pressure, wind patterns, and the influence of the monsoon system.

The seasonal cycle plays a major role in agriculture, water availability, economic activities, and daily life.

## Classification of Seasons

The India Meteorological Department (IMD) recognizes four major seasons.

## Winter Season

### Period

- January to February

### Characteristics

- Cool and dry weather
- Low temperatures in northern India
- Snowfall in Himalayan regions
- Clear skies in many areas

### Importance

- Supports rabi crops
- Tourism in hill stations
- Snow accumulation in the Himalayas

## Summer Season

### Period

- March to May

### Characteristics

- Rising temperatures
- Heat waves in northern and central India
- Formation of low-pressure areas
- Dry conditions in many regions

### Local Winds

Important local winds include:

- Loo in northern India
- Dust storms in northwestern India

## Southwest Monsoon Season

### Period

- June to September

### Characteristics

- Main rainy season of India
- Arrival of moisture-laden winds
- Majority of annual rainfall received during this period

### Branches of the Monsoon

#### Arabian Sea Branch

Brings rainfall to:

- Western Ghats
- Western Coast
- Central India

#### Bay of Bengal Branch

Brings rainfall to:

- Northeastern India
- Ganga Plains
- Eastern India

### Importance

- Supports agriculture
- Recharges rivers and reservoirs
- Provides water for industries and households

## Retreating Monsoon Season

### Period

- October to December

### Characteristics

- Withdrawal of southwest monsoon winds
- Declining rainfall in most regions
- Cyclonic activity in the Bay of Bengal

### Northeast Monsoon

The northeast monsoon provides significant rainfall to:

- Tamil Nadu
- Coastal Andhra Pradesh
- Parts of southeastern India

## Seasonal Importance

India's seasons influence:

- Agriculture
- Water resources
- Biodiversity
- Energy demand
- Transportation
- Economic activity

## Seasonal Challenges

### Winter

- Cold waves
- Fog conditions

### Summer

- Heat waves
- Water shortages

### Monsoon

- Floods
- Landslides
- Urban flooding

### Retreating Monsoon

- Cyclones
- Coastal flooding

## Relationship with Agriculture

### Kharif Crops

Depend heavily on monsoon rainfall.

Examples:

- Rice
- Maize
- Cotton

### Rabi Crops

Grow during winter.

Examples:

- Wheat
- Mustard
- Barley
