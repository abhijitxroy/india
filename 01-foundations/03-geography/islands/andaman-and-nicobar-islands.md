# Andaman and Nicobar Islands

## Overview

The Andaman and Nicobar Islands are a group of islands located in the Bay of Bengal. They form a Union Territory of India and are strategically important because of their location near major international sea routes in the Indian Ocean region.

The islands are known for their rich biodiversity, tropical forests, indigenous communities, and maritime significance.

## Location

The islands are situated in the southeastern part of the Bay of Bengal.

They lie closer to Southeast Asia than to mainland India and occupy an important position near the Malacca Strait.

## Administrative Status

The Andaman and Nicobar Islands are administered as a Union Territory of India.

### Capital

- Port Blair

### Administrative Features

- Largest island Union Territory of India
- Comprises hundreds of islands and islets
- Governed by a Lieutenant Governor

## Island Groups

The Union Territory consists of two major island groups.

### Andaman Islands

Located in the northern part of the territory.

Important islands include:

- North Andaman
- Middle Andaman
- South Andaman
- Little Andaman

### Nicobar Islands

Located in the southern part of the territory.

Important islands include:

- Car Nicobar
- Great Nicobar
- Little Nicobar

## Important Geographical Features

### Indira Point

Indira Point is the southernmost point of India.

It is located on Great Nicobar Island.

### Saddle Peak

Saddle Peak is the highest point in the Andaman and Nicobar Islands.

### Barren Island

Barren Island contains India's only active volcano.

### Narcondam Island

Narcondam Island is of volcanic origin and is known for its unique biodiversity.

## Climate

The islands experience a tropical climate.

Characteristics include:

- High humidity
- Warm temperatures throughout the year
- Heavy rainfall
- Influence of both southwest and northeast monsoons

## Biodiversity

The islands are among India's richest biodiversity regions.

### Flora

Major vegetation includes:

- Tropical evergreen forests
- Mangrove forests
- Coastal vegetation

### Fauna

Important wildlife includes:

- Saltwater crocodiles
- Sea turtles
- Dugongs
- Nicobar pigeon
- Andaman wild pig

## Indigenous Communities

Several indigenous tribal groups inhabit the islands.

Examples include:

- Great Andamanese
- Jarawa
- Onge
- Sentinelese
- Nicobarese

These communities possess unique cultural traditions and lifestyles.

## Strategic Importance

The islands are among India's most strategically important territories.

Reasons include:

- Location near the Malacca Strait
- Presence in the Indian Ocean region
- Maritime surveillance capabilities
- Naval and air force operations
- Regional security importance

### Andaman and Nicobar Command

India's only integrated tri-service military command is located in the islands.

It includes participation from:

- Indian Army
- Indian Navy
- Indian Air Force

## Environmental Challenges

Major challenges include:

- Tsunamis and earthquakes
- Coastal erosion
- Climate change impacts
- Coral reef degradation
- Biodiversity conservation concerns
