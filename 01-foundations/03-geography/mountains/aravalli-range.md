# Aravalli Range

## Overview

The Aravalli Range is one of the oldest fold mountain systems in the world and among the oldest mountain ranges in India.

It extends across northwestern India and plays an important role in climate, ecology, mineral resources, and environmental conservation.

The range acts as a natural barrier between the Thar Desert and the fertile plains of northern India.

## Location

The Aravalli Range extends from Gujarat through Rajasthan to Haryana and Delhi.

Major states and regions include:

- Gujarat
- Rajasthan
- Haryana
- Delhi

## Formation

The Aravalli Range was formed during ancient geological processes that occurred billions of years ago.

Over time, weathering and erosion have significantly reduced its height.

Today, it appears as a series of residual hills rather than very high mountains.

## Physical Features

### Length

The Aravalli Range extends for approximately 700 kilometres.

### Orientation

The range runs in a southwest to northeast direction.

### Elevation

The mountains are relatively low compared to the Himalayas.

## Highest Peak

### Guru Shikhar

- Highest peak of the Aravalli Range
- Located in Mount Abu, Rajasthan
- Height: Approximately 1,722 metres

## Important Regions

### Mount Abu

The only major hill station in Rajasthan.

It is known for:

- Pleasant climate
- Forests
- Wildlife
- Dilwara Temples

### Delhi Ridge

The northern extension of the Aravalli Range.

It acts as an important green zone for the National Capital Region.

## Climate Significance

The Aravalli Range influences regional climate by:

- Reducing desert expansion towards the east
- Influencing local rainfall patterns
- Supporting groundwater recharge
- Acting as a natural ecological barrier

## Biodiversity

The range supports diverse flora and fauna.

### Vegetation

Common vegetation includes:

- Dry deciduous forests
- Thorn forests
- Scrub vegetation

### Wildlife

Important species include:

- Leopard
- Hyena
- Jackal
- Nilgai
- Various bird species

## Mineral Resources

The Aravalli region is rich in minerals.

Important minerals include:

- Copper
- Zinc
- Lead
- Marble
- Granite

Mining has historically been an important economic activity in the region.

## Environmental Importance

The Aravallis contribute to:

- Soil conservation
- Groundwater recharge
- Biodiversity protection
- Desertification control
- Air quality improvement

## Environmental Challenges

Major challenges include:

- Illegal mining
- Deforestation
- Urbanization
- Habitat fragmentation
- Land degradation

Conservation of the Aravallis is important for ecological sustainability in northwestern India.
