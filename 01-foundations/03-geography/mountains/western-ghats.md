# Western Ghats

## Overview

The Western Ghats are a mountain range running parallel to the western coast of India.

They are one of the most important physical features of the Indian peninsula and are recognized as a global biodiversity hotspot.

The Western Ghats play a major role in climate regulation, monsoon rainfall, river systems, biodiversity conservation, and water security.

## Location

The Western Ghats extend from the Tapti River valley in Gujarat to the southern tip of India in Tamil Nadu and Kerala.

Major states covered include:

- Gujarat
- Maharashtra
- Goa
- Karnataka
- Kerala
- Tamil Nadu

## Formation

The Western Ghats are part of the ancient Peninsular Plateau.

They were formed through geological processes associated with the breakup of the Gondwana supercontinent.

## Physical Features

### Length

The Western Ghats extend for approximately 1,600 kilometres.

### Elevation

The average elevation ranges from 900 to 1,600 metres.

The range rises steeply from the western coastal plains.

## Highest Peak

### Anamudi

- Highest peak in the Western Ghats
- Located in Kerala
- Height: 2,695 metres

## Important Hill Ranges

Major hill ranges include:

- Nilgiri Hills
- Anaimalai Hills
- Cardamom Hills
- Sahyadri Range

## Climate Significance

The Western Ghats strongly influence India's monsoon climate.

### Orographic Rainfall

Moist winds from the Arabian Sea rise along the mountain slopes and produce heavy rainfall.

As a result:

- Western slopes receive heavy rainfall
- Eastern side lies in a rain-shadow region

## River Systems

Many important peninsular rivers originate in the Western Ghats.

Examples include:

- Godavari
- Krishna
- Kaveri
- Tungabhadra
- Periyar

These rivers support agriculture, industries, and settlements.

## Biodiversity

The Western Ghats are recognized as one of the world's biodiversity hotspots.

### Vegetation

Important vegetation types include:

- Tropical evergreen forests
- Tropical moist deciduous forests
- Montane forests
- Shola grasslands

### Wildlife

Important species include:

- Lion-tailed Macaque
- Nilgiri Tahr
- Asian Elephant
- Tiger
- Leopard
- Malabar Giant Squirrel

## UNESCO World Heritage Site

Several protected areas within the Western Ghats have been designated as UNESCO World Heritage Sites.

The region is internationally recognized for its ecological significance.

## Protected Areas

Important protected areas include:

- Silent Valley National Park
- Periyar National Park
- Bandipur National Park
- Nagarhole National Park
- Eravikulam National Park

## Economic Importance

The Western Ghats contribute through:

- Agriculture
- Plantation crops
- Hydroelectric power
- Tourism
- Water resources

Major plantation crops include:

- Tea
- Coffee
- Rubber
- Cardamom
- Pepper

## Environmental Challenges

Major challenges include:

- Deforestation
- Habitat fragmentation
- Mining
- Encroachment
- Climate change
- Human-wildlife conflict
