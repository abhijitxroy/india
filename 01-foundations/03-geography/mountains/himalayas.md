# Himalayas

## Overview

The Himalayas are the youngest and highest mountain range in the world.

They form a natural barrier along India's northern frontier and play a crucial role in climate, river systems, biodiversity, agriculture, defence, and regional geography.

The Himalayas are often referred to as the 'Water Tower of Asia' because many major rivers originate from this mountain system.

## Formation

The Himalayas were formed due to the collision of the Indian Plate with the Eurasian Plate.

This tectonic collision began millions of years ago and continues even today.

As a result, the Himalayas are still rising gradually.

## Location

The Himalayan mountain system extends across northern India and several neighbouring countries.

In India, the Himalayas pass through:

- Jammu and Kashmir
- Ladakh
- Himachal Pradesh
- Uttarakhand
- Sikkim
- West Bengal
- Arunachal Pradesh

## Physical Features

### Length

Approximately 2,400 kilometres.

### Width

Ranges from about 150 to 400 kilometres.

### Elevation

Contains many of the world's highest peaks.

## Major Divisions of the Himalayas

### Himadri (Greater Himalayas)

Characteristics:

- Northernmost range
- Highest peaks
- Permanent snow cover
- Major glaciers

### Himachal (Lesser Himalayas)

Characteristics:

- Lower than Himadri
- Famous hill stations
- Dense forests
- Important valleys

### Shiwalik (Outer Himalayas)

Characteristics:

- Southernmost range
- Lowest elevation
- Formed from river deposits
- Broad valleys known as Duns

## Important Peaks

### Kanchenjunga

- Highest peak in India
- Located on the India-Nepal border

### Nanda Devi

- Highest peak entirely within India

Other important peaks include:

- Kamet
- Saltoro Kangri
- Saser Kangri

## Glaciers

Important Himalayan glaciers include:

- Gangotri Glacier
- Yamunotri Glacier
- Siachen Glacier
- Zemu Glacier

These glaciers are major sources of freshwater.

## River Systems

Many important rivers originate in the Himalayas.

Examples include:

- Ganga
- Yamuna
- Brahmaputra
- Indus
- Sutlej

These rivers support hundreds of millions of people.

## Climate Importance

The Himalayas influence India's climate by:

- Blocking cold winds from Central Asia
- Helping monsoon rainfall formation
- Influencing weather patterns
- Supporting water security

## Biodiversity

The Himalayas support rich biodiversity.

### Vegetation

- Alpine vegetation
- Coniferous forests
- Temperate forests
- Subtropical forests

### Wildlife

Important species include:

- Snow Leopard
- Red Panda
- Himalayan Black Bear
- Musk Deer
- Himalayan Monal

## Economic Importance

The Himalayas contribute through:

- Tourism
- Hydroelectric power
- Agriculture
- Horticulture
- Forest resources

Major crops include:

- Apples
- Tea
- Saffron
- Various fruits and vegetables

## Strategic Importance

The Himalayas are extremely important for India's national security.

They form natural boundaries with:

- China
- Nepal
- Bhutan

Important border regions include:

- Line of Actual Control (LAC)
- Siachen Region
- Arunachal Pradesh Sector

## Environmental Challenges

Major challenges include:

- Glacier retreat
- Climate change
- Landslides
- Deforestation
- Floods
- Unplanned development
