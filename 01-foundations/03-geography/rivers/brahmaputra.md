# Brahmaputra River

## Overview

The Brahmaputra is one of the major river systems of Asia and one of the most important rivers of India.

It is known for its enormous water volume, wide river channel, fertile floodplains, and significant role in the geography and economy of northeastern India.

The river is unique because it flows through Tibet, India, and Bangladesh.

## Origin

The Brahmaputra originates from the Angsi Glacier near Lake Manasarovar in Tibet.

In Tibet, the river is known as the Yarlung Tsangpo.

It flows eastward across the Tibetan Plateau before entering India.

## Course of the River

### Tibet

The river flows as the Yarlung Tsangpo through southern Tibet.

### India

The river enters India through Arunachal Pradesh.

In Arunachal Pradesh, it is known as the Siang or Dihang.

After entering Assam, it becomes the Brahmaputra.

### Bangladesh

The river enters Bangladesh where it is known as the Jamuna.

It later joins the Ganga (Padma) and Meghna river systems before entering the Bay of Bengal.

## Length

The total length of the Brahmaputra River is approximately 2,900 kilometres.

## Major Tributaries

### Left Bank Tributaries

- Dibang
- Lohit
- Burhi Dihing
- Dhansiri

### Right Bank Tributaries

- Subansiri
- Kameng
- Manas
- Sankosh

These tributaries contribute significantly to the river's water volume.

## Brahmaputra Valley

The Brahmaputra Valley is one of India's most fertile regions.

### Importance

- Agriculture
- Fisheries
- Transportation
- Biodiversity
- Human settlements

## Majuli Island

Majuli, located in Assam, is one of the world's largest river islands.

It is culturally and ecologically significant.

## Economic Importance

The Brahmaputra supports:

- Agriculture
- Inland water transport
- Fisheries
- Hydroelectric power generation
- Tourism

The river is particularly important for Assam's economy.

## Biodiversity

The Brahmaputra Basin supports rich biodiversity.

Important ecosystems include:

- Floodplain wetlands
- Grasslands
- Forests

Important wildlife includes:

- One-horned Rhinoceros
- Asian Elephant
- Bengal Tiger
- Gangetic Dolphin

## Floods

The Brahmaputra is well known for annual flooding.

### Causes

- Heavy monsoon rainfall
- Snowmelt
- High sediment load
- Numerous tributaries

### Impact

- Crop damage
- Erosion
- Displacement of people
- Infrastructure damage

At the same time, floods replenish soil fertility.

## Strategic Importance

The Brahmaputra is important for:

- Water security
- Hydropower development
- Border region development
- Regional connectivity

Its upper course in Tibet also makes it important in India's relations with China.

## Environmental Challenges

Major challenges include:

- Flooding
- Riverbank erosion
- Climate change
- Glacier retreat
- Habitat degradation

## Delta Formation

The Brahmaputra joins the Ganga and Meghna river systems to form the Sundarbans Delta.

This is the largest delta in the world.
