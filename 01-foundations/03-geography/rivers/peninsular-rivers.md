# Peninsular Rivers

## Overview

Peninsular rivers are the river systems that originate in the Peninsular Plateau of India.

These rivers are generally older than the Himalayan rivers and flow through stable geological regions.

Most peninsular rivers depend primarily on rainfall and have a more seasonal flow pattern.

## Characteristics

Major characteristics of peninsular rivers include:

- Older river systems
- Flow through hard rock terrain
- Mostly rain-fed rivers
- Comparatively shorter courses
- Fixed river channels
- Lower risk of course changes

## Origin

Most peninsular rivers originate from:

- Western Ghats
- Central Highlands
- Peninsular Plateau

The Western Ghats serve as the source of many major rivers.

## Drainage Pattern

Peninsular rivers drain into two major water bodies.

### Bay of Bengal Drainage

Most peninsular rivers flow eastward and drain into the Bay of Bengal.

Examples:

- Godavari
- Krishna
- Kaveri
- Mahanadi

### Arabian Sea Drainage

Some rivers flow westward into the Arabian Sea.

Examples:

- Narmada
- Tapi (Tapti)
- Mahi
- Sabarmati

## Major Peninsular Rivers

### Godavari

- Longest peninsular river
- Known as the 'Dakshin Ganga'
- Flows into the Bay of Bengal

### Krishna

- Second-longest peninsular river
- Originates in the Western Ghats

### Kaveri

- Important river of southern India
- Supports extensive irrigation

### Mahanadi

- Important river of eastern India
- Forms a fertile delta in Odisha

### Narmada

- Flows westward
- Flows through a rift valley
- Empties into the Arabian Sea

### Tapi (Tapti)

- West-flowing river
- Runs parallel to the Narmada for part of its course

## Economic Importance

Peninsular rivers support:

- Irrigation
- Drinking water supply
- Hydroelectric projects
- Agriculture
- Industries
- Fisheries

Many major dams have been built on these rivers.

## Important Dams and Projects

Examples include:

- Hirakud Dam (Mahanadi)
- Nagarjuna Sagar Dam (Krishna)
- Sardar Sarovar Dam (Narmada)
- Krishnaraja Sagar Dam (Kaveri)

## Difference Between Himalayan and Peninsular Rivers

| Feature | Himalayan Rivers | Peninsular Rivers |
|----------|----------|----------|
| Age | Younger | Older |
| Water Source | Glaciers and Rainfall | Mainly Rainfall |
| Flow | Perennial | Seasonal |
| Terrain | Young Mountains | Stable Plateau |
| Sediment Load | Higher | Lower |

## Challenges

Major challenges include:

- Water scarcity during dry seasons
- River pollution
- Inter-state water disputes
- Over-extraction of water
- Climate variability
