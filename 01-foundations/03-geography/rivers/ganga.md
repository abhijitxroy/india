# Ganga River

## Overview

The Ganga is the most important river of India and one of the most significant river systems in the world.

It is revered as a sacred river in Hinduism and plays a central role in India's history, civilization, agriculture, economy, and culture.

The Ganga basin supports one of the largest concentrations of population in the world.

## Origin

The Ganga originates from the Gangotri Glacier in Uttarakhand.

The river emerging from the glacier is known as the Bhagirathi.

At Devprayag, the Bhagirathi meets the Alaknanda River and from this point onward the river is known as the Ganga.

## Course of the River

The Ganga flows generally from northwest to southeast across northern India.

Major states through which it flows include:

- Uttarakhand
- Uttar Pradesh
- Bihar
- Jharkhand
- West Bengal

After entering Bangladesh, it is known as the Padma.

## Length

The total length of the Ganga River is approximately 2,525 kilometres.

## Major Tributaries

### Left Bank Tributaries

- Ramganga
- Gomti
- Ghaghara
- Gandak
- Kosi

### Right Bank Tributaries

- Yamuna
- Son
- Tamsa

These tributaries contribute significantly to the river's water volume.

## Ganga Basin

The Ganga Basin is the largest river basin in India.

### Importance

- Supports agriculture
- Provides drinking water
- Supports industries
- Enables transportation
- Sustains biodiversity

## Major Cities on the Ganga

Important cities located along the river include:

- Rishikesh
- Haridwar
- Kanpur
- Prayagraj
- Varanasi
- Patna
- Kolkata

## Confluence Points

### Devprayag

Confluence of:

- Bhagirathi
- Alaknanda

### Prayagraj

Confluence of:

- Ganga
- Yamuna
- Saraswati (traditionally believed)

This confluence is known as the Triveni Sangam.

## Delta Formation

The Ganga joins the Brahmaputra and Meghna river systems before entering the Bay of Bengal.

Together they form the Sundarbans Delta, the largest delta in the world.

## Economic Importance

The Ganga supports:

- Agriculture
- Fisheries
- Inland waterways
- Tourism
- Religious activities

The fertile alluvial plains of the Ganga basin are among the most productive agricultural regions in India.

## Religious and Cultural Importance

The Ganga is considered sacred by millions of people.

Major religious activities include:

- Pilgrimages
- Ritual bathing
- Festivals
- Religious ceremonies

Important pilgrimage centres include Haridwar, Prayagraj, and Varanasi.

## Environmental Challenges

Major challenges include:

- Water pollution
- Industrial waste
- Sewage discharge
- Plastic pollution
- Encroachment

## Conservation Efforts

Important initiatives include:

- Namami Gange Programme
- River cleaning projects
- Wastewater treatment infrastructure
- Biodiversity conservation efforts
