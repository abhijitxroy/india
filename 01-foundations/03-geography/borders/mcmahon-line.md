# McMahon Line

## Overview

The McMahon Line is the boundary line between India and China in the eastern sector of the India-China border.

It forms the boundary between Arunachal Pradesh in India and the Tibet Autonomous Region of China.

India recognizes the McMahon Line as the legal international boundary in the eastern sector, while China does not accept it.

## Historical Background

### Simla Convention (1914)

The McMahon Line was proposed during the Simla Convention of 1914.

The convention involved representatives of:

- British India
- Tibet
- China

### Sir Henry McMahon

The boundary line was named after Sir Henry McMahon, the British Indian Foreign Secretary who played a key role in drawing the boundary.

## Location

The McMahon Line runs along the Himalayan region between:

- Arunachal Pradesh (India)
- Tibet (China)

It extends for approximately 890 kilometers.

## Significance

### India-China Border

The McMahon Line forms the basis of India's claim regarding the eastern sector of the India-China boundary.

### Arunachal Pradesh

India considers Arunachal Pradesh an integral part of the country.

China claims parts of Arunachal Pradesh, leading to differences over the border.

### Strategic Importance

The region is important because of:

- National security considerations
- Border management
- Military deployments
- India-China relations

## China's Position

China does not recognize the McMahon Line.

China argues that Tibet did not possess the authority to independently negotiate international boundary agreements at the time.

This difference remains one of the major issues in the India-China border dispute.

## Relationship with the Line of Actual Control

The McMahon Line and the Line of Actual Control (LAC) are not identical.

- The McMahon Line is a proposed international boundary.
- The LAC represents the present-day line of military control.

Both concepts are important for understanding the India-China border dispute.

## Geographic Features

The McMahon Line passes through:

- Himalayan mountains
- High-altitude valleys
- Forested regions
- Remote border areas

The terrain makes border management challenging.

## Strategic Challenges

Major challenges include:

- Border disputes
- Difficult terrain
- Infrastructure development
- Military tensions
- Differing interpretations of the boundary

## Importance for India

The McMahon Line is important because it:

- Defines India's eastern boundary claim with China
- Supports border administration in Arunachal Pradesh
- Influences defence planning
- Shapes India-China diplomatic discussions
