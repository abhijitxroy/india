# Buddhism

## Overview

Buddhism is one of the major religions that originated in ancient India. It was founded by Gautama Buddha in the sixth century BCE as a response to social and religious conditions of the time. Buddhism emphasized ethical conduct, self-discipline, and the path to liberation from suffering.

From India, Buddhism spread across Asia and influenced the history, culture, philosophy, art, and architecture of many civilizations.

## Historical Background

### Conditions Before Buddhism

During the sixth century BCE:

- Ritualism had become prominent in religious practices.
- Society was influenced by rigid social divisions.
- New philosophical ideas were emerging in the Gangetic plains.
- Many thinkers questioned existing traditions and sought alternative paths.

Buddhism emerged as part of this wider intellectual movement.

## Gautama Buddha

### Early Life

- Born as Siddhartha Gautama.
- Born at Lumbini.
- Belonged to the Shakya clan.
- Raised in a royal environment.

### Four Sights

According to tradition, Siddhartha encountered:

1. An old man
2. A sick man
3. A dead person
4. A wandering ascetic

These experiences led him to seek answers about human suffering.

### Enlightenment

- Left his home in search of truth.
- Practised meditation and asceticism.
- Attained enlightenment under the Bodhi Tree at Bodh Gaya.
- Became known as the Buddha, meaning "The Enlightened One".

### Mahaparinirvana

- Delivered teachings across northern India.
- Died at Kushinagar.
- His death is known as Mahaparinirvana.

## Core Teachings

### Four Noble Truths

1. Life involves suffering (Dukkha).
2. Suffering is caused by desire and attachment.
3. Suffering can be ended.
4. The path to end suffering is the Eightfold Path.

### Eightfold Path

- Right View
- Right Intention
- Right Speech
- Right Action
- Right Livelihood
- Right Effort
- Right Mindfulness
- Right Concentration

### Middle Path

Buddha advocated a Middle Path that avoided both extreme luxury and extreme asceticism.

## Spread of Buddhism

### Role of Ashoka

Emperor Ashoka played a major role in spreading Buddhism after the Kalinga War.

His efforts included:

- Patronage of Buddhist institutions
- Missionary activities
- Construction of stupas
- Promotion of Dhamma

### Expansion Beyond India

Buddhism spread to:

- Sri Lanka
- Central Asia
- China
- Korea
- Japan
- Southeast Asia
- Tibet

## Buddhist Literature

### Tripitaka

The Buddhist canon is known as the Tripitaka.

It consists of:

- Vinaya Pitaka
- Sutta Pitaka
- Abhidhamma Pitaka

## Art and Architecture

Major contributions include:

- Stupas
- Chaityas
- Viharas
- Rock-cut caves
- Buddhist sculptures

Important centres:

- Sanchi
- Bharhut
- Amaravati
- Ajanta
- Ellora

## Decline in India

Factors often associated with decline include:

- Revival of Hindu traditions
- Internal divisions
- Reduction of royal patronage
- Foreign invasions affecting institutions
