# Jainism

## Overview

Jainism is one of the oldest religions originating in India. It emphasizes non-violence, self-discipline, truth, and spiritual purification. Jain traditions trace their teachings through a succession of spiritual teachers known as Tirthankaras.

Jainism played an important role in shaping Indian philosophy, ethics, literature, art, and culture.

## Historical Background

### Origins

Jain traditions believe that the religion is eternal and was taught by a series of Tirthankaras.

The twenty-fourth and last Tirthankara of the present cycle was Mahavira, who reorganized and strengthened the Jain community during the sixth century BCE.

### Religious Environment

During the sixth century BCE:

- New philosophical movements emerged across northern India.
- Ritual-based practices were increasingly questioned.
- Thinkers explored alternative paths to liberation.
- Jainism and Buddhism emerged as influential traditions.

## Mahavira

### Early Life

- Born as Vardhamana.
- Associated with the Kshatriya clan.
- Born in the region of present-day Bihar.
- Later became known as Mahavira.

### Renunciation

- Left worldly life in search of spiritual truth.
- Practised severe austerities and meditation.
- Attained Kevala Jnana (supreme knowledge).

### Teachings

Mahavira emphasized:

- Non-violence (Ahimsa)
- Truth (Satya)
- Non-stealing (Asteya)
- Non-possession (Aparigraha)
- Celibacy (Brahmacharya)

## Core Principles

### Ahimsa

Ahimsa is the most important principle of Jainism. It teaches complete non-violence towards all living beings.

### Three Jewels

The path to liberation is based on:

1. Right Faith
2. Right Knowledge
3. Right Conduct

### Karma and Liberation

Jainism teaches that:

- Actions create karma.
- Karma binds the soul.
- Liberation is achieved by removing karmic bondage.
- A liberated soul attains Moksha.

## Jain Literature

Important Jain texts include:

- Agamas
- Kalpa Sutra
- Tattvartha Sutra

These texts preserve religious teachings, philosophy, and ethical principles.

## Sects of Jainism

### Digambara

- Monks traditionally do not wear stitched clothing.
- Greater emphasis on ascetic practices.

### Svetambara

- Monks wear white clothing.
- Preserved a separate textual tradition.

Both sects share the fundamental teachings of Jainism.

## Spread of Jainism

Jainism spread through:

- Royal patronage
- Trade networks
- Monastic communities
- Merchant groups

Important centres developed across western and southern India.

## Art and Architecture

Major contributions include:

- Jain temples
- Rock-cut monuments
- Sculptures of Tirthankaras
- Religious manuscripts

Important sites:

- Mount Abu
- Shravanabelagola
- Palitana
- Ranakpur

## Contribution to Indian Culture

Jainism influenced:

- Ethics and non-violence
- Literature
- Architecture
- Trade communities
- Philosophical thought

Its emphasis on Ahimsa later influenced several social and political movements in India.
