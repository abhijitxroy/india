
# British Raj

## What Was the British Raj?

The British Raj was the period during which the British Crown directly ruled most of the Indian subcontinent.

It lasted from 1858 to 1947.

The British Raj began after the Revolt of 1857 and ended when India gained independence on 15 August 1947.

## What Existed Before It?

Before the British Raj:

- The East India Company controlled large parts of India.
- Company rule expanded through wars, treaties, and annexations.
- Discontent grew among soldiers, rulers, farmers, and citizens.

The Revolt of 1857 convinced Britain that India should be governed directly by the Crown.

---

## Beginning of the British Raj

In 1858:

- The East India Company's rule ended.
- The British Crown assumed direct control.
- A Viceroy was appointed to govern India on behalf of the British monarch.

This marked the beginning of the British Raj.

---

## Administrative Structure

### British Monarch

The King or Queen of Britain remained the highest authority.

### Viceroy of India

The Viceroy represented the British Crown in India.

Important Viceroys included:

- Lord Canning
- Lord Curzon
- Lord Irwin
- Lord Mountbatten

### Provinces and Princely States

India consisted of:

- British-administered provinces
- Princely states ruled by local rulers under British supervision

---

## Major Developments

### Railways

The British expanded railway networks across India.

Impact:

- Faster transportation
- Improved trade connectivity
- Easier movement of goods and people

### Telegraph and Postal Systems

Communication became faster across the subcontinent.

### Modern Bureaucracy

Many modern administrative institutions emerged during this period.

Examples:

- Civil services
- Police systems
- Courts
- Revenue administration

---

## Economic Impact

### Positive Developments

- Railways expanded.
- Ports developed.
- Modern banking institutions emerged.
- Large administrative systems were created.

### Criticisms

- Wealth was extracted from India.
- Traditional industries weakened.
- Famines caused widespread suffering.
- Economic policies often favored British interests.

The economic legacy of British rule remains a subject of debate.

---

## Social and Educational Changes

Important developments included:

- Modern universities
- English education
- Expansion of newspapers
- Social reform movements

Many future freedom fighters emerged from these educational institutions.

---

## Rise of Indian Nationalism

During the British Raj:

- Political awareness increased.
- Nationalist movements expanded.
- Demands for self-rule grew stronger.

The founding of the Indian National Congress in 1885 became a major milestone.

---

## Major Freedom Movements

### Non-Cooperation Movement

Led by Mahatma Gandhi.

### Civil Disobedience Movement

Included the Salt March.

### Quit India Movement

Launched in 1942 demanding immediate British withdrawal.

These movements increased pressure on British rule.

---

## Partition of Bengal (1905)

The British divided Bengal in 1905.

This decision triggered widespread protests and strengthened nationalist sentiment.

The partition was later reversed in 1911.

---

## Jallianwala Bagh Massacre (1919)

British troops opened fire on a gathering at Jallianwala Bagh in Amritsar.

The incident shocked the nation and intensified demands for independence.

---

## Why Did British Rule End?

Several factors contributed:

- Growth of nationalism
- Freedom movements
- Economic pressures
- World War II impacts
- Increasing demand for self-government

By the 1940s, British control became increasingly difficult to maintain.

---

## End of the British Raj

In 1947:

- British rule ended.
- India became independent.
- Partition created India and Pakistan.

This marked the end of nearly two centuries of British political dominance.

---

## Significance

The British Raj influenced:

- Administration
- Legal systems
- Railways
- Education
- Infrastructure
- Politics

It also gave rise to the modern Indian independence movement.

---

## Quick Revision

- Duration: 1858–1947.
- Began after the Revolt of 1857.
- Governed directly by the British Crown.
- Viceroy represented British authority in India.
- Railways, bureaucracy, and legal institutions expanded.
- Nationalist movements gained strength.
- Ended with Independence and Partition in 1947.
