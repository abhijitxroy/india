
# Judiciary of India

## Overview

The Judiciary is one of the three pillars of the Indian constitutional system.

It is responsible for interpreting laws, resolving disputes, protecting rights, and ensuring that the Constitution remains supreme.

An independent judiciary is essential for democracy, rule of law, and protection of citizens.

---

## Why Study the Judiciary?

Studying the judiciary helps understand:

- Rule of law
- Constitutional governance
- Protection of rights
- Dispute resolution
- Checks and balances
- Judicial independence

The judiciary acts as the guardian of the Constitution.

---

## Three Pillars of Government

```text
Legislature
     ↓
Makes Laws

Executive
     ↓
Implements Laws

Judiciary
     ↓
Interprets Laws
```

Each institution performs a distinct constitutional function.

---

## Judicial Structure

```text
Supreme Court
      ↓
High Courts
      ↓
District Courts
      ↓
Subordinate Courts
```

The judiciary functions through a hierarchical court system.

---

## Major Functions

### Interpretation of Laws

- Clarifies legal provisions
- Resolves legal disputes
- Ensures consistent application of laws

### Protection of Rights

- Safeguards constitutional rights
- Provides legal remedies
- Reviews government actions

### Judicial Review

```text
Law or Action
       ↓
Court Review
       ↓
Constitutional Test
       ↓
Valid / Invalid
```

Courts can examine whether actions comply with the Constitution.

### Dispute Resolution

- Civil disputes
- Criminal cases
- Constitutional matters
- Administrative disputes

---

## Importance of Judicial Independence

An independent judiciary helps ensure:

- Fair trials
- Equal treatment under law
- Protection from misuse of power
- Public confidence in institutions

---

## Judiciary and Democracy

Citizens

The judiciary helps maintain constitutional democracy and the rule of law.
